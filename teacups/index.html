What you are operating

This is not a chart of a tea-cup ride — it is one, seen from straight above. A heavy brass platter turns at rate Ω. Bolted to it, offset by R, is a cup that turns on its own pin at rate ω relative to the platter. You sit at offset ρ inside the cup. Your seat's path in the world is the sum of two circular motions, z(t) = R·e^{iΩt} + ρ·e^{i(Ω+ω)t} — a rose. Drag the outer ring to crank the platter, drag the inner cup to crank its pin; let go and each coasts (the platter, being heavier, coasts longer).

The bloom — one petal per numerator

Write the spin ratio in lowest terms, ω/Ω = p/q. The flower closes — the seat returns exactly to its start — only after the platter has turned q whole times, at T = 2π·q/Ω. Over that one closed loop it grows exactly p petals: the numerator, not the denominator. (A common re-telling gets this backwards; the self-test settles it — 1/2 blooms 1 petal, 2/3 blooms 2, 3/5 blooms 3, 5/7 blooms 5.) As you coast near a clean rational the cup eases into a magnetic detent and the on-screen tally announces the reduced fraction and its petal count.

The G-needle & the tea — the felt pull

Your body doesn't feel position, it feels acceleration: a(t) = z″(t) = −Ω²R·e^{iΩt} − (Ω+ω)²ρ·e^{i(Ω+ω)t}. Its magnitude swings between two bounds as the two spins line up or oppose. The brass G-needle reads |a| live; an engraved coral LURCH arc marks the maximum R·Ω²+ρ(Ω+ω)² (both pulls add — the cusp of the flower, where you're flung outward) and a teal FLOAT arc marks the minimum |R·Ω²−ρ(Ω+ω)²| (the pulls oppose — the crown, where you ease). Re-cranking re-cuts both arcs. The cup of tea leans toward the felt pull and tells you the direction: it climbs the wall and flashes coral at the lurch, and goes flat with a teal weightless shimmer at the float.

Lock the cup — the proof by touch

Pull LOCK THE CUP (or press L) and ω snaps to zero: the cup stops turning on its pin. Now the two spins co-rotate at the same rate, the seat just rides a single circle of radius R+ρ, the petal count falls to 0, and the G-needle goes dead steady at a constant (R+ρ)·Ω² — the tea sits perfectly level. No flower, no swing. Release the lock and flick the cup, and the petals bloom and the needle swings again. That is the load-bearing negative control, and you can feel it.

Closure is rational

The flower closes for some finite time iff ω/Ω is rational. Crank toward an irrational ratio like √2/2 and the seat never returns to its start — it fills an annulus forever, the trail never quite joining up. The self-test proves there is no return within a safe tolerance over the first 2000 candidate periods for √2/2, while every rational closes to machine precision.

Honesty

The closure, the petal count, and the felt extrema are exact — read straight from the core's closurePeriod, petalCount, and extrema. A point-mass rider, rigid arms, no friction or drift; the spins advance at constant rates Ω and Ω+ω. Only the on-canvas scale of the drawing and the coast damping are nominal so the ride reads on a finite stage; the trail is the seat's true world path, sampled finely. The cranks clamp to ±9 rad/s so you can sit on both signs and watch the petals form either way.
