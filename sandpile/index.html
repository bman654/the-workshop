The Sandpile

Pour grains onto a grid. A cell holding four can't hold them — it topples, spilling one grain to each neighbour, which may topple their neighbours, and so an avalanche crosses the whole pile. Here is the strange part you can watch with your own eyes: topple the cells in any order you like — first to last, last to first, pure chaos — and the sand always lands on the same floor. That order-freedom is a theorem, and it turns these heaps into a group with a single eerie identity.

Empirical · the avalanche-size distribution

A log–log scatter of how often an avalanche of size s occurs over a long rain. It looks straight-ish — a power-law fit, not a theorem — and it bends down at the right: the grid is finite, so the biggest avalanches are clipped (the finite-size cutoff). The order-freedom above is the proven part of this page; this is the honestly-empirical part.
