What this bench proves — exactly

By enumeration (the grid). With an informed host the nine worlds split clean: in the 6 worlds where your first pick is a goat (off the diagonal), the host is forced to reveal the other goat, so the unopened door is the car — switch wins. In the 3 worlds where you picked the car (the diagonal), switching loses. So P(win | switch) = 6/9 = 2/3 and P(win | stay) = 3/9 = 1/3, exactly — area you can count.
By Bayes (the swing). Given your pick, the host opening that specific goat door happens with probability ½ when your pick is the car (he had two goats to choose from) but probability 1 when your pick is a goat (forced). The posterior on the other unopened door is therefore (1·⅓)/(1·⅓ + ½·⅓) = 2/3. Two independent derivations, one answer.
The neg-control. Flip to the careless host — he opens a random unpicked door, sometimes the car. Those rounds are voided (no goat to switch toward); count only the goat-revealed rounds and the grid reshuffles to 3/6 = 1/2. The advantage vanishes — it was bought by what the host knew, not by the wood.

The Belief Beam · the same evidence, weighed
The host's reveal is one piece of evidence, and the door it spares is the one it makes you believe in. There, belief pours between glass vials as a posterior that always sums to one; here, that same Bayes step is a door you can open with your hand. Two faces of conditional probability.
