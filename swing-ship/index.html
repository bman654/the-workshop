That single rhythm pumps the pendulum's length at twice its natural beat — and the arc grows on its own. Time it right and you reach the loop; mistime it and the swing dies. Why the length, pumped at 2×, is the whole trick

A swing held at a fixed length L is a plain pendulum: it rocks at ω₀ = √(g/L) and slowly dies. But change the length as you swing and a new term appears in the equation of motion: −(2L̇/L)·θ̇. Shorten the rod (stand up) while you are moving fastest and that term feeds the swing; lengthen it (crouch) at the turning points and you lose nothing. Do this twice per swing — once at each end — and the length is being pumped at ωₘ = 2ω₀, exactly twice the swing's own frequency.

That 2:1 ratio is special. Linearize the motion and it becomes Mathieu's equation, whose principal instability tongue sits right at ωₘ = 2ω₀. Inside the tongue the amplitude grows exponentially with no outside push — this is parametric resonance, the deep sibling of ordinary driven resonance.

The proof is exact and self-checking. The pumped pendulum over one beat is a linear, periodic map — its monodromy matrix M. Because the length returns to itself each beat, det(M) = 1 exactly. Its eigenvalue |λ| is the verdict: at 2×ω₀, |λ| > 1 — the swing climbs at rate σ = ln|λ|/Tₘ; at 1×ω₀, |λ| = 1 — bounded forever. Same effort, opposite outcome. The amplitude rail at right is only a witness — the real readout is the arc you are riding.
