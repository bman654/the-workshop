The Coaster
the loop you dare to shape · energy & the 2.5r law

Shape the rail, set the loop's radius, pull the hoist lever — and the bead runs your track under gravity. It either whips over the top or peels off the rail and falls. The split is one number, and it is geometry: a loop survives only when you drop from at least 2½ loop-radii up. Watch the energy slosh, watch the rail-force needle peg to zero the instant the car detaches.

the rider's-frame cockpit

The same loop — from inside the car. Sit in the seat, feel the felt weight press and lift as the rail bends you: heavy through the valley, nearly weightless at the crest of a just-legal run. A first-person cockpit on the very same physics as The Coaster.

The Rotor
gravity drops, you stick to the wall

Step into a spinning drum; it whirls up to speed and then the floor drops away — and you do not. Friction against the wall, fed by the spin's centripetal press, holds you pinned. Find the spin rate where the floor can safely fall.

The Drop Tower
the room fell with you, so the scale reads nothing · apparent weight N=m(g+a)

Hoist a sealed cabin up the mast and drop it — and the camera rides with you: the room holds still while the world streaks up past the windows. A loose coin lifts off the floor and hangs; the brass scale-needle pegs to zero. The room fell with you, so the scale reads nothing — until the brake slams it to the crush. Two spatial knobs trade drop height against the brake band; the crush is one exact, monotone number: peak g = h/d + 1.
