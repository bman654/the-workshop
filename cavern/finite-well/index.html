Self-test — the proof

Now give the box a real, finite depth — walls a particle can climb if it has the energy. Two things break that the infinite box never let you see: the ladder is now finite (a well only holds so many rungs — deepen or widen it and more snap into being), and because the walls are climbable the wave doesn't stop at the edge — it leaks out and decays into the forbidden ground beyond. The shallowest rung, clinging just below the rim, leaks the farthest.

Compare it to the box and the bowl next door. The infinite box held an endless ladder (∝ n²) and its wave died dead at the wall; here the walls are only so tall, so only a handful of rungs fit beneath the rim, and each one's wave bleeds through the wall and decays with a length 1/κ set by how far below the rim it sits. Drag the depth down toward 20 and watch the top rungs evaporate one by one — a state too shallow to bind simply ceases to exist. There is, however, always at least one bound state, no matter how shallow the well. The self-test proves each rung against an independent from-scratch eigensolve of the actual stepped potential.
