Newton's gravity draws a closed ellipse — the planet retraces the very same oval forever. But Mercury's doesn't quite close: its perihelion (its nearest point to the Sun) creeps forward a little each orbit, tracing a slow rosette. The leftover — 43 arc-seconds a century that no Newtonian tug could account for — was the first triumph of General Relativity. Turn the GR correction on and watch the ellipse start to walk.
