Self-test — the proof

Now soften the walls. Instead of a box, hang the particle in a parabolic bowl — the potential of a perfect spring, the curve every system falls into near a stable point. The energy is still quantized, but the ladder changes its shape: the rungs become perfectly even, spaced by a single quantum ℏω, and the lowest one still hovers a half-step above zero. And because the bowl is soft, the wave leaks past the walls — into ground the particle classically could never reach. Pick a rung; then launch a coherent state and watch a quantum packet swing like a pendulum, never spreading.

Compare it to the box next door: there the rungs grew as n², here they march up in equal steps — that even ladder is exactly why a spring (or a vibrating bond, or a photon's field) absorbs and emits light in identical quanta. The lowest rung is ½ℏω, not zero: even at absolute zero a quantum spring must jitter. And the soft bowl lets the wave tunnel past its own turning points — the ground state spends ~16% of its time where a classical ball could never be. Launch a coherent state — a ground state nudged sideways — and it swings back and forth like a marble in the bowl, rigid and un-spreading: the closest a quantum thing comes to being classical. The self-test proves the even ladder against a from-scratch finite-difference eigensolve of −½∂²+½ω²x² — a different algebra than the formula.
