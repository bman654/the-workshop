Self-test — the proof

Pinch a slit shut and the beam fights back. The narrower you pin the particle's position Δx at the slit, the more its sideways momentum Δp must fan out on the screen — and the product Δx·Δp can never drop below ħ/2. A soft Gaussian slit rides exactly on that floor, kissing it for every width. A hard top-hat slit, at the same Δx, sits stubbornly above it — its sharp edges pay a sinc-tail tax. Squeeze the jaws and watch the trade happen.

Flip to Hard and the needle sits above the floor with visible side-lobes in the band — the sinc tax you cannot squeeze away.

Scope & honesty. Natural units ħ = 1, so the floor is the literal 0.500. Δx and Δp are RMS second moments of |A(x)|² and |Ã(k)|² about their (zero) means — that is what makes "= ħ/2 exactly" unambiguous. The Gaussian is the minimum-uncertainty state and saturates the bound in closed form for every width. The top-hat is the load-bearing counter-example: its hard edges put weight in arbitrarily high modes, so Δp is the RMS over the diffraction pattern's central lobe (window k∈[−K,K], K=π/a, m=1) — a finite, scale-invariant number, strictly above the floor. The needle reads the exact core number, never a value re-measured from the rendered speckle (the speckle is the picture; the needle is the truth).
