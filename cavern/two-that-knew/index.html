Self-test — the proof

Two coins are minted as one entangled pair and fly to opposite stations. You set each analyzer's dial; each coin lands ±1. Average enough pairs and the agreement E(a,b) = −cos(a−b) emerges — and the assembled S sweeps a needle past a wall at S=2 that no pre-painted coin can cross, up to the quantum limit 2√2 ≈ 2.83. Flip the source to pre-paint the coins and watch the same needle slam into that wall.

Set the four dials, then RUN 500 pairs. Watch two things: the trays under each station stack ±1 chips (aligned dials → mirror-opposite trays, every pair — that is the anti-correlation), and the big agreement-needle climbs. With the source in quantum mode the needle sweeps past the brass wall at S=2 and seats at 2.83. Flip pre-paint the coins and an amber hard-stop appears at the wall: the needle slams into S=2 and cannot pass for any dials. Same wall, opposite fate — that is Bell's theorem you can touch.

Scope & honesty. The coins are a spin-½ singlet; outcomes are ±1. E(a,b) is the true correlation −cos(a−b), computed two independent ways inside the sole core (the closed form and the Born-rule expectation ⟨ψ⁻|σ(a)⊗σ(b)|ψ⁻⟩) — they agree to machine ε, so E is the Born rule, not an assumed curve. The pre-paint control is the fairest local hidden-variable strategy (each coin reads only its own dial + a shared source instruction); its S is capped at exactly 2 — proved both by the exhaustive 16-case sign algebra and a swept dial grid. The needle reads the exact core S; the four E rows are the receipts converging from the tallies.
