The Cavern

The estate's physics laboratory, quarried underground and kept apart from the house — the dangerous experiments quarantined below for safety. The shaft forks into two drifts: a warm-lamplit Newtonian one and a cold, star-lit Einsteinian one. Every bench is a single self-contained instrument that proves its own physics exact.

Dangerous physics · handle with care

The Newtonian Drift

force · momentum · the old certainties

The lamplit gallery, where the world runs on Newton's clockwork: balls collide and nothing is lost, and the curve of fastest descent turns out to be a wheel's own track.

⚙️
Newton's Cradle
Lift a ball, let it fall; the click-clacks carry momentum and energy across the line untouched. Vary the count, the masses, the bounce.
conserves p & KE to 1e-12 🔥
The Maxwell–Boltzmann Gas
Hundreds of hard discs bounce in a box, every collision conserving momentum and energy exactly. Start them all at one speed and watch the spike relax by itself into the bell-shaped Maxwell–Boltzmann curve — order distilled from random collisions. The first bench of heat in the cave.
live χ² fit to M–B · rejects a delta-spike, accepts equilibrium · exact conservation 🧱
Where Pressure Comes From
No balloon, no piston — just the same hard discs drumming on the walls. Each bounce flips one velocity component and kicks the wall by 2m|v⊥|; add up those kicks per unit wall, per unit time, and out falls a pressure that settles onto P·A = N·kT — the exact law the Carnot engine merely assumes. Squeeze the box and watch P climb to hold the line.
P from wall-bounces → N·kT/A live · same law Carnot assumes · Δp=2m|v⊥| exact to 1e-12 🛝
The Brachistochrone on the workbench
Race four beads from A to B — and the cycloid wins. Its twin the tautochrone drops beads from any height and they all arrive together. (Built earlier; it lives on the Workbench, but it belongs here too.)
the cycloid is provably fastest

The Einsteinian Drift

light · spacetime · the deep dark

The cold gallery, deeper in, where the certainties bend: a clock made of light runs slow as it moves, and gravity nudges a planet's orbit forever onward.

⏱️
The Light Clock
Push a bouncing-photon clock toward the speed of light and watch it slow. Time dilation, length contraction, and the invariant interval — drawn from one slanted ray.
γ from Pythagoras, exact ☿
Mercury's Precession
Newton's orbit closes; Einstein's does not. Dial in the relativistic correction and watch the perihelion creep — the famous 43″ a century that first proved General Relativity.
lands on 43″/century

The Quantum Drift

the very small · superposition · the watched answer

Past where the certainties bent, the cave goes stranger still: here a single thing travels every path at once, and the simple act of looking changes what happens. The deepest, most dangerous physics in the estate — and, as ever, each bench proves it exact.

🎯
The Double Slit
Fire particles one at a time at a pair of slits; each lands as a single dot, yet the dots pile into bright and dark bands. Close a slit — or merely watch which one it takes — and the bands vanish. The experiment Feynman called the whole mystery of quantum mechanics.
fringe spacing λL/d, exact · Born-rule |ψ|² ⟨⟩
Two Wings, One Slit
The double slit and the Hall's diffraction grating look like different physics — two slits versus a thousand, a quantum particle versus a beam of light. They are the same equation: one dimensionless array factor, read at N = 2 and at N ≫ 2. Grow N and watch cos² sharpen without rising; send an electron and a photon through one apparatus and prove their fringes byte-identical, joined by λ = h/p.
arrayFactor(2) === cos² · electron λ(p) === photon λ, byte-identical ⇅
Spin — the Stern–Gerlach Split
A silver beam never smears — it splits into exactly two spots; prepare a state, block one beam, tilt the next magnet and watch the split obey the angle alone; re-measure the first axis and the purity is gone — measuring x erased z.
P(↑)=cos²(θ/2)=½(1+n̂·m̂), two derivations agree to ε · crossed analyzer exactly ½ · seeded 200k → target ±binomial · classical-deflection control fails 🔗
The Two That Knew
Mint one entangled pair and send the two coins to opposite analyzer stations; operate the dials and fire pairs, and the agreement-needle climbs past a wall at S=2 that no pre-painted coin can cross, up to the quantum limit 2√2. Then flip the source to pre-paint the coins at birth and watch the same needle slam into that very wall — Bell's theorem you can touch.
E(a,b)=−cos(a−b), two derivations agree to ε · CHSH→2√2 at 0/90/45/135 · LHV capped at 2 (16-case algebraic + swept grid) · the gap >0.8 💣
The Bomb That Tells On Itself
A single photon can tell you a bomb is live without ever touching it. In a balanced interferometer the photon always exits the bright port — until you drop an Elitzur–Vaidman bomb (a perfect detector) into one arm. Fire photons one at a time: ½ go boom, but ¼ of the time the forbidden dark port clicks — and a dark click is impossible without a bomb. That click proves the bomb is live, by a photon that took the empty arm and never went near it.
no bomb ⇒ P(bright)=1, P(dark)=0 · live bomb ⇒ P(boom)=½, P(bright)=¼, P(dark)=¼ to ε · P(dark)>0 iff a bomb is present (FP rate 0) · Σp=1 at every stage 👁
The Watched Coin
A coherent drive would turn a qubit clean from |0⟩ to |1⟩ — a full flip. But peek at it along the same axis again and again and you keep catching it still at |0⟩: each look collapses the tiny drift back to the pole. Crank the dial from a few peeks to 128 and watch the flip freeze out — a watched pot never boils. The same single-peek projector the Stern–Gerlach bench proves, multiplied N times.
P(stay)=cos²ᴺ(π/2N) closed form === a product of N reused spin projectors to <1e-12 · N=0/1 ⇒ full flip (neg-control) · rotate-don't-collapse ⇒ 0 · P_flip→π²/4N ⛰️
Quantum Tunnelling
Roll a particle at a wall it hasn't the energy to climb. Classically it always bounces back — but the wavefunction leaks through, and a sliver comes out the far side. Widen the wall and the leak dies exponentially; that fragility is why α-decay half-lives span 24 orders of magnitude.
transmission T closed-form == transfer-matrix solve · R + T = 1 exact 📦
Particle in a Box
Trap a particle between two walls it cannot pass and its energy turns quantized — a ladder of rungs spaced by n², with no rung at zero, so even at rest it must hum. Climb the rungs, count the nodes, then mix two and watch the bound packet slosh. The root of why atoms have lines, not a smear.
ladder E_n ∝ n² == a from-scratch eigensolve · orthonormal to 1e−15 🌀
The Harmonic Oscillator
Soften the box into a parabolic bowl — a quantum spring. The energy is still a ladder, but now the rungs are perfectly even (spaced by one quantum ℏω, the lowest still ½ above zero), and the wave leaks past the walls into ground a classical ball could never reach. Launch a coherent state and a quantum packet swings like a pendulum, never spreading.
even ladder E_n = ℏω(n+½) == a from-scratch eigensolve · coherent state swings classically 🕳️
The Finite Well
Give the box a real, finite depth — walls a particle can climb. Now the ladder is finite (a well holds only so many rungs; deepen it and more snap in, shallow it and the top ones evaporate — but it always binds at least one), and the wave leaks out through the walls and decays into the forbidden ground beyond, the shallowest rung reaching farthest. The realistic well, between the box and the bowl.
finite ladder from a transcendental match == a from-scratch eigensolve · exponential leak ⛓️
The Lattice
Now line the wells up in a row, repeated forever — a crystal. The atom's sharp levels can no longer stay sharp: each one smears into a band of allowed energies, and between the bands open forbidden gaps no electron may have. Where the bands and gaps fall is the whole reason a solid is a metal, a semiconductor, or an insulator.
band edges where |f(E)|≤1 == ½·tr M(E) transfer-matrix · N states per band from a ring eigensolve 🔔
Hear the Ladder
You've climbed two ladders — the box's rungs spreading as n², the oscillator's perfectly even. Now listen to them. Voiced as sound, the oscillator's even rungs are the overtone series of a single note, so they fuse into one pure pitch — that is why it is called harmonic. The box's stretching rungs never line up, so it rings like a struck bell. The same physics you saw, finally heard.
oscillator inharmonicity == 0 (one tone) · box stack provably stretches (a bell) · the Sound Garden meets the Cavern ⟳
The Stubborn Spinner
Grab a spinning molecule's energy dial and try to set any rate — it refuses, snapping to its allowed shelves. A rigid rotor's energies are ℓ(ℓ+1) = 0, 2, 6, 12, 20… so the gaps widen as you climb, an even comb you can hear rising. Each shelf is really 2ℓ+1 states at once. A neg-control lamp separates this true ladder from a near-miss ℓ² cheat — the box's n² in disguise.
E_ℓ=ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1)/2I · gaps 2,4,6,8 (even comb) · 2ℓ+1 orientations · the ratio test separates it from the box's n² cheat ⚛
The Hydrogen Atom
The real cloud of where the electron is — drag to rotate it. The round s, the dumbbell p, the cloverleaf d are each a true shape, with dark gaps where the electron is never found. The quiet rail is its energy: the s, p, d of a shell sit on the exact same rung, a degeneracy only the −1/r well has. Screen the charge and the coincidence breaks apart.
ψ = R(r)·Y(θ,φ), a rotatable |ψ|² cloud · radial ladder == a from-scratch eigensolve · angular Y_lm orthonormal & node-count exact · s/p/d coincide (the Coulomb accident) 🌊
The Wave Packet
Every bench so far froze a state in place. Now let one move. Launch a Gaussian packet and watch it travel — the bump crawls at the group velocity while the colour, the phase, races ahead at half the speed. Spread it free, slosh it in a bowl, or fire it at a wall too tall to climb and watch a piece tunnel through — the same wall, finally seen happening.
unitary evolve ∫|ψ|²=1 to 1e-9 · ⟨x⟩ rides group velocity v_g=k₀ (Ehrenfest) · transmitted lobe == static T(E_mean) ⊟
The Squeeze
Pinch a slit shut and the beam fights back. The tighter you pin the particle's position at the gap, the wider its sideways momentum must fan across the screen — and the product never falls below ħ/2. A soft Gaussian slit rides exactly on that floor, kissing it for every width; a hard top-hat slit, at the same width, sits stubbornly above it — paying a sinc-tail tax its sharp edges cannot shed. Drag the jaws and watch the trade happen.
Gaussian Δx·Δp = ħ/2 to ε · top-hat at same Δx strictly larger · swept widths never < ħ/2
