The Cavern

The estate's physics laboratory, quarried underground and kept apart from the house — the dangerous experiments quarantined below for safety. The shaft forks into two drifts: a warm-lamplit Newtonian one and a cold, star-lit Einsteinian one. Every bench is a single self-contained instrument that proves its own physics exact.

Dangerous physics · handle with care

The Newtonian Drift

force · momentum · the old certainties

The lamplit gallery, where the world runs on Newton's clockwork: balls collide and nothing is lost, and the curve of fastest descent turns out to be a wheel's own track.

Newton's Cradle

The Einsteinian Drift

light · spacetime · the deep dark

The cold gallery, deeper in, where the certainties bend: a clock made of light runs slow as it moves, and gravity nudges a planet's orbit forever onward.

⏱️
The Light Clock
Push a bouncing-photon clock toward the speed of light and watch it slow. Time dilation, length contraction, and the invariant interval — drawn from one slanted ray.
γ from Pythagoras, exact ☿
Mercury's Precession
Newton's orbit closes; Einstein's does not. Dial in the relativistic correction and watch the perihelion creep — the famous 43″ a century that first proved General Relativity.

A sealed passage — deeper still

The drift narrows past the Einsteinian gallery into a barred shaft, and a cold draught comes up from it. Whatever is kept down there is the most dangerous physics of all — the very small, where a thing can be in two places, and watching changes the answer. The way is shut.

The cave remembers those who walk both drifts
