↤ Its discrete cousin — The Warren

Two pursuits, one lesson: exploit the constraint, not your speed. The Warren reads where you stand — a monster walks a fixed beat it can never break, and you cross on the rhythm its back is turned, exploiting its timing. The Homicidal Chauffeur reads when you jink — a car twice your speed bends no tighter than its turn radius, and you slip through the wide overshoot that limit forces, exploiting its turning. A discrete clock and a continuous curve — the same move.

The kinematics, proved before you ever steered

The car and the proof move by ONE function — advance(s, κ, ds), the exact constant-curvature arc. The car's shortest path between two posed (point, heading) states under its turn limit is the closed-form Dubins path; we check the six words land on the goal and that their length equals the forward-integrated arc to machine epsilon, cross-check against an independent Newton-shooting oracle, and confirm an RK4 of the raw unicycle ODE converges to the same arc. Then the pursuit itself: a straight flee is caught in bounded time, the optimal jink survives at the real R, and the same jink is caught when R→0 — the turning constraint, not the speed, is the lever.
