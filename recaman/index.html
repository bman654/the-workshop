a greedy little rule Start at 0. At step n, try to step back by n: if that lands on a non-negative number you've never visited, take it — otherwise step forward by n. That one rule traces OEIS A005132 (Bernardo Recamán, 1991). Drawn as semicircles hopping the number line, the footsteps interlock.

Where Recamán is a greedy walk, the spiral is a greedy layout — light only the primes and they fall onto diagonals. Two ways the plain counting numbers turn out to have a hidden shape.
What you scrub vs what is proven. The scrubber replays a recorded chain of exact integer states. The proof — every step is ±n and every retreat lands on fresh ground — lives in the chip and in core.mjs's self-test, mirrored in core.test.mjs. Throw the lever and the chip turns red because the engine produces the violation: the discipline belongs to the rule, not the picture.

True: magnitude ±n · non-negative · retreats never land on old ground. Honest: Recamán's forward jumps DO sometimes revisit a value — e.g. a(24)=a(20)=42 — so the folk-claim "it never repeats" is false. What's special is that it never retreats onto old ground.
