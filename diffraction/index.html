The Diffraction Grating

the shape of light's shadow is a Fourier transform

the far screen — the diffraction pattern you'd see
intensity I(sinθ) — closed form vs the direct Fourier transform

Light passing an opening doesn't cast a sharp shadow — it fans out into fringes. In the far field the pattern is the squared Fourier transform of the aperture's shape. One slit gives a sinc; a row of N slits — a grating — multiplies it by a sharp interference comb whose bright orders obey d·sinθ = mλ.

Drag the plot to read off any angle. Raise N and the bright orders sharpen (half-width ∝ 1/N) — that's why a grating out-resolves two slits. When the pitch is a whole multiple of the width, some orders vanish: missing orders, killed by the slit's own zero. Switch to white light and each order fans into a spectrum — a grating is a spectrometer.
