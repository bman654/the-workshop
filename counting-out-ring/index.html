The Counting-Out Ring

A schoolyard rhyme empties a ring of chairs — eeny, meeny… you're OUT — and which seat is left standing is not luck. It is fixed math. Bet the chair you think survives, lock it, and watch every k-th seat ash to grey until one remains. At k = 2 you can read the answer straight off N's binary: slide the leading 1 to the end and the bits spell the survivor. For k = 3 and up there is no such shortcut — only the count decides.

leading 1 slides to the end → survivor = 2L+1 = 19
general k has no binary crown — the survivor comes only from running the count (the live recurrence).

The Josephus problem: count out by k around a ring of N and one seat survives — pure recurrence, J(N,k) = (J(N−1,k) + k) mod N, with J(1)=0 and survivor = J+1. What you watch on the ring is the same simulation the self-test certifies against that recurrence, so the cull on screen cannot drift from the theorem.
