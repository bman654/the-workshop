The Binary Ruler

The optimal way to solve a Tower of Hanoi IS counting 0,1,2,… in binary. The disc you move at step t is fixed by the ruler sequence; the tower lands home at exactly 2n−1 moves. So solve it and the brass odometer rolls to its ceiling — the number is the trophy. The whole peg-config is read straight off t by a closed-form Gray decoder; the single digit it changes names the disc that moves. Stray off the ruler and a provable floor-meter rolls your wheel past the brass — overshoot you can see.

You-drive: click a peg to lift its top disc, click another peg to drop it. Each on-ruler move rolls the brass with you; an off-ruler move sends your wheel past the optimal floor — the floor-meter reddens and tells you the best you can still do.

Reading the instrument

Optimal floor — the tower homes at exactly 2ⁿ−1 moves; the brass pip marks it.
Overshoot — your count rolls past the brass floor; the meter shows +N above it and the best you can do now.
Carry / name — a wheel rolls over; the Gray digit that changes (and the lamp that flips) names the disc to move next.
