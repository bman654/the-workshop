What is singing

A songbird has no larynx. It has a syrinx, at the fork where the windpipe splits, and it has two of them — one on each bronchus, worked by separate muscles. Each is a pair of soft labia the bird pushes air past. Below a threshold they sit still and the air goes by in silence; above it they flap, and the flap chops the airflow into a tone.

The model here is the low-dimensional one from the birdsong-physics literature. In a time nondimensionalised by the labial rate constant γ, the labial midpoint x obeys

dx/dτ = u
du/dτ = −α − βx − x³ + x² − (x² + x)u

α is the pressure in the air sac; β is how tight the bird holds the labia. γ is nothing but the clock — it turns τ back into seconds, and so sets the bird's register. That is the entire instrument. Six birds, one equation, different curves.

Where it sings, and where it cannot

Equilibria have u = 0 and satisfy x³ − x² + βx + α = 0, with Jacobian trace −(x² + x) and determinant β + 3x² − 2x. Three things follow with no simulation in them at all:

The Hopf line. The trace vanishes at x = 0, which the cubic forces to α = 0. Left of it the quiet state is stable and nothing can sound. The eigenvalues there are ±i√β, so a note is born at

f = γ √β / 2π

The fold. The quiet state can also be destroyed outright, by colliding with a saddle. Double roots satisfy the cubic and 3x² − 2x + β = 0 together, which parametrises the fold by its own double root: β = 2x − 3x², α = 2x³ − x², for x from ½ to ⅔. It runs from (0, ¼) to (4/27, 0). Right of it there is no quiet state anywhere, so the bird must sing.

The roof. The trace also vanishes at x = −1, which forces α = β + 2. Blow harder than that and the labia are simply held open. Silence again.

Between the Hopf line and the fold — only below β = ¼ — a quiet state and a song can both exist, and which you get depends on where you came from. The Fluter's notes lie in there, which is why they snap on instead of fading in: a saddle-node does not have a small amplitude to start at.

What is honest about it, and what is not

The pitch formula is a threshold claim and the room does not pretend otherwise. Push α up and the limit cycle grows, the oscillation stops being sinusoidal, and the note runs sharp: 2 % at α = 0.05, 25 % at α = 0.20, half an octave by α = 0.5. That is why these birds sing at low pressure — it is where tension really is the pitch — and it is a constraint the model imposed on the composing, not one that was designed in.

The six birds are not identifications and not recordings. Nothing here was fitted to a field tape. They are six curves a hand drew in two numbers, named for the shape they make. The claim is not that this is a blackbird; the claim is that all six of them, and anything you draw, come out of one equation with two knobs.

Two voices

Both syringes empty into one trachea, so their partials add before the tube and beat with each other. The Chatterer holds its two sides deliberately out of tune — 3180 Hz on the left, 3800 Hz on the right, a ratio of 1.195, which is nobody's interval. Shut one side and that pitch leaves the spectrum by 147 dB while the other stays exactly where it was. That is a thing one throat can do and two throats cannot: sing a chord with itself.
