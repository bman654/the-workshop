how do we know — the math we claim, and the one we don't

A line at rest wavelength λ_rest seen from a star moving at v (redshift positive) is observed at λ_obs = λ_rest·(1 + v/c) — the classical low-v Doppler, the form this page renders and the game inverts exactly: v = c·Δλ/λ_rest. Every Balmer line shifts by the same fraction, so the comb slides rigid — the self-test proves all four lines recover the identical v. We cap the speed at ±0.05c for two honest reasons: it keeps the whole comb (Hδ 410 blued to ~390, Hα 656 redded to ~689) inside the visible band so the plate never loses a line; and at 0.05c the classical form agrees with the relativistic longitudinal Doppler λ_obs = λ_rest·√((1+β)/(1−β)) to ~1.4% — faithful. We show that relativistic correction in the readout below, we just don't claim it. The warm/cool tint is a mood cue, not a claim — the line positions carry the physics.
