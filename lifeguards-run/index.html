The Lifeguard's Run

least-time · refraction
checking…

A lifeguard sprints over fast sand, then swims through slow water, to reach a swimmer. Running straight at them wastes time fighting the slow water; running to the water's edge first wastes distance on land. The quickest route is bent — and the bend it settles into is Snell's law. Drag the brass grommet where the path crosses the shore (or tap anywhere on the bright shoreline) and watch the stopwatch race toward the floor.

At the least-time crossing, dt/dx = 0 — and that exact condition, written with the geometry, IS sinθ₁/v₁ = sinθ₂/v₂: Snell's law. The bench proves it — the time-minimizing crossing satisfies Snell to a billionth, and that it's a minimum (not merely stationary) is the whole point.
