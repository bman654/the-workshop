Four crossing rods, one crank —
a line scored straight by inversion.
colophon — the wonder this bench holds

The seventh brass drawing-engine of The Drawing Room, and the second linkage in the estate to draw an exact straight line — but where The Straightedge spends Peaucellier's six bars, Hart's inversor spends only four. It is a crossed antiparallelogram: two long bars a and two short bars b, assembled crossed. Three points ride the bars at a shared fraction s: O the grounded pole, P the crank point, Q the pen. Hart's theorem: O, P, Q stay collinear and OP·OQ = (a²−b²)·s(1−s) is constant — P is the circle-inverse of Q about O. Drive P on a crank circle through O and Q inks a line so straight it reads as a scored fault in the paper.

A grounded point that sits mid-bar is rank-deficient — the two bars to its rod-endpoints are collinear with it, and a naïve solve drifts the line to ~10⁻². So each carrier bar is drawn as a rigid brass gusset plate (a triangle with one off-line apex) and O and P are located by three bars to the three plate vertices. Non-degenerate — the locus falls to machine-ε. The drawn machine still reads as four crossing rods and a crank; the gussets are the honest representation, not a fudge.
