What you are operating

This is not a chart of a wall-of-death — it is one, seen in front cutaway. Two riders stand against the inside wall of a drum of radius r. When it spins at rate ω, the wall must shove each rider inward to bend them onto their circular path: that inward shove is the normal force, the wall press N = mω²r. The only thing fighting gravity once the floor is gone is friction along the wall, and friction can supply at most μN. The rider stays pinned exactly when that reserve carries the full weight: μN ≥ mg. Cancel the mass and the condition is μω²r ≥ g — the threshold is ωc = √(g/μr), and there is no mass in it. That is why the heavy adult and the light child stick or sink together.

The crank — one honest verb

The brass crank (and its slider twin) sets ω and nothing else. The drum's banded wall sweeps faster as you turn it; the tachometer needle is ω, and an engraved red ωc mark sits at the threshold — it physically moves when you change μ or r. Once the needle climbs past that mark, the wall can hold. Then pull DROP THE FLOOR: the hinged brass plate swings down on its hinge, baring the shaft. Above ωc both riders hang pinned over the void; below it both slump and sink — and they read the same sink, because the slip acceleration g − μω²r is mass-free too.

The balance dial — the race you watch cross

The side dial is the inequality made into a race. A fixed tick engraved gravity · mg marks the weight to be carried; a filling column — the friction reserve μN — climbs as you crank. While the column is short the dial reads reserve < weight — you SLIDE; the instant it reaches the tick it latches reserve ≥ weight — you STICK, and a gold hairline marks the ωc crossing. No plotted curve — just the two bars racing, and the moment one overtakes the other.

The frictionless-wall control

Flip on frictionless wall (μ = 0) and crank all you like: the wall still presses — the press bar N = mω²r grows just the same, you can feel the crush — but the friction reserve μN is dead at zero. So the riders slide at every spin, the dial never latches, and the ωc mark vanishes off the tachometer to +∞. That is the neg-control you can feel: spin presses; only friction holds. The self-test proves holdsFrictionless ≡ false across every spin — including spins above the real ωc, where the true wall holds — a genuine, non-empty disagreement.

Honesty

The pin/slide threshold and the wall press are exact: the riders' fate (pinned vs sinking) is read straight from the core's holds and riderState. Only the slide's draw is nominal — the sink uses a fixed fall depth so it reads on a finite canvas, and the drop is eased a few frames so the floor swings rather than teleports. Idealized: point-mass riders, a rigid drum wall, dry Coulomb friction with a single coefficient μ (no static/kinetic split, no air drag). The crank tops out at ω = 6 rad/s so you can sit on both sides of the ωc ≈ 3.30 crossing.
