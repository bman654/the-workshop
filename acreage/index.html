The Acreage

A surveyed yard, parcelled into plots. Each brass stone names the area of its plot — and there is exactly one way the plots tile the whole field with no gap and no overlap. Drag a stone to stretch its claim; the tag flips teal the instant the rectangle's height × width equals the area. A claim never destroys anything — the yard can only be unfinished, never lost.

Tap a claimed plot to release it. Keyboard: Tab to a stone, Enter to claim, arrows to stretch, Enter to latch, Esc to cancel.
A plot's area alone is a weak fact — many rectangles share it. But laid in a field where every cell must be owned exactly once, the areas conspire: the clues admit exactly one tiling, and pure logic — only-fit and cell-forced — reaches it with no guessing. Because Σ(areas) = the field's area, a full claim is necessarily a perfect tiling. The badge proves it across many yards.  ·  Back to the Numbers Room
