Uphill With Their Eyes Closed

a blind swarm · a fingertip of scent · the bloom that floods to it
core checking…

A warm dish of loam under a grow-lamp, seeded with a haze of blind spores. Drag a fingertip and you sow nectar-scent — a soft amber plume soaking into the agar. No spore can see it. Each only knows whether the scent where it sits is getting sweeter or sourer than a moment ago. Yet within seconds the haze tears into rivers and floods toward your fingertip, etching its migration as living moss-vein trails. Sow a second spot and the swarm splits in two. Rake the dish and it loses its way. The whole bloom is one local rule, summed over hundreds of the blind.

No spore can see which way is uphill. Each one only knows one thing — getting sweeter? run on. getting sourer? cast about. That, summed over hundreds of the blind, is the bloom.

the watched spore

One spore wears a ring. Hover anywhere to lock the ring onto the nearest one and trace its recent path — bright where it ran, kinked where it tumbled. No cell ever aims; watch how it stumbles uphill.

what you're seeing

Amber is the scent you painted. Bright chartreuse filaments are spores on a good run (climbing); dim churn is spores still searching. Where they pool, the peak ignites — then shimmers, cells forever failing to leave a summit.

What you're watching. Bacterial run-and-tumble chemotaxis: each spore runs in a straight line, and tumbles to a fresh random heading at a rate that rises when its scent is dropping and falls when its scent is climbing. It never reads the gradient in space — only its own scent one step ago. The bias lives entirely in run length, never in aim, so the reorientations point nowhere in particular — and still the crowd floods uphill. Blind them sets that gain to zero, collapsing the rule to an aimless random walk on the same food; the bloom dissolves. These are a clean teaching dish's numbers, not a claim about real cells. The green pill and core.test.mjs prove the payoff fires — the migration, not a theorem.
