The Selection Jar

directional selection · a Conservatory bench
core checking…

A jar of beetles you can SEE, on a floor you tint. Each carries a heritable shade; a predator eats the most-visible beetle each generation; the survivors breed offspring a shade like their parents. Turn the floor dark and RUN the generations — the salt-and-pepper cloud crawls until nearly every beetle is the colour you chose and the jar goes quiet. Its sibling the Gene Jar won’t drift; this one does — camouflage you watch evolve, not an allele table.

What’s proven vs what you’re watching. The living jar illustrates selection — a creature evolving into invisibility in front of you. What is proven exact is the breeder’s equation R = h²·S on the ensemble mean over many seeded runs (within a 3·SE band), confirmed a second way by an independent regression slope = h². Both neg-controls behave: set h² = 0 and the cloud will NOT track despite identical culling; turn selection off (random predation) and it only drifts by sampling noise, never toward the floor. The proof lives in the green self-test pill and in core.test.mjs.
