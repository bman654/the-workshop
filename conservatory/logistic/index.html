Real cells on a glass dish bud and divide while there is room, then visibly jam as they crowd toward the carrying capacity K — until no cell has room left to bud. Nobody draws the S-curve; it emerges from a population you can count. On the back glass their own census paints the faint ghost sigmoid the logistic law predicts. Resize the dish, overcrowd it, or switch to greedy feeding and watch it ring.

The dish is a camera — every printed number reads the proven core, and the back-glass census paints the exact closed-form sigmoid the cells are recovering.
