▦ The Warren — The Crossing

The estate's second game engine. Lantern's verb was carry a thing; the Warren's verb is where you stand. One monster walks a fixed deterministic patrol — it never reacts to you. Stillness is a move: step, wait, and cross the corridor on the beat its back is turned.

Keys: WASD/arrows to step, Space to wait, R to reset. Each time you act, the monster takes exactly one step by its fixed rule. The dark band is lethal only while the patrol stands in it — read the rhythm.

The solver's verdict — proved before you ever stepped

SURVIVABLE — a safe step/wait interleaving exists

A turn-based grid with a deterministic monster is a finite game tree. A headless breadth-first search over canonical (x, y, phase mod period) states proves whether any sequence of steps and waits reaches the exit without ever sharing a hazard tile with the patrol — and a reverse-reachability pass proves you can never get stranded alive (no softlock). The negative-control floor is built so the patrol's period tiles the only crossing — every crossing is caught — so the solver returns NO SAFE PATH and the floor would be rejected, never shipped. That it can say no is the proof it isn't rubber-stamping yes. The same proof runs in a headless Node twin, byte-for-byte the same engine.

This is one floor, one monster — the Warren's first room. Its natural next floors are documented in the SPEC: a reactive chaser, and stateful pressure-plate gates.

↦ Its continuous cousin — The Homicidal Chauffeur

Two pursuits, one lesson: exploit the constraint, not your speed. The Warren reads where you stand — a monster walks a fixed beat it can never break, and you cross on the rhythm its back is turned, exploiting its timing. The Homicidal Chauffeur reads when you jink — a car twice your speed bends no tighter than its turn radius, and you slip through the wide overshoot that limit forces, exploiting its turning. A discrete clock and a continuous curve — the same move.

Through the door, the same crossing runs on a curve. Here a monster walks a clock you learn to read; behind this door a car twice your speed can turn no tighter than its radius — and you slip out through the wide overshoot that limit forces. A discrete beat and a continuous arc, the one move.
