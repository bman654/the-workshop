Number · the constant π

Two Ways to π

one number-line · one number reached two opposite ways.

Down the middle runs one π number-line, fixed from 3.0 to 3.5, with 3.14159… inscribed along it. On that single line live both answers. The gold spell is a count: two blocks on a frictionless lane clack exactly 3, 31, 314, 3141 times — an integer, locked at π from the first frame, never moving. The stammer is a guess: matchstick-needles rain on a planked floor (green where they cross a seam, grey when clean) and the fraction that cross drops π out of thin air — a white caret riding the same ruler inside a teal corridor that narrows like 1/√N. Drop until it lands: the corridor shrinks until it first straddles the gold bar — the moment the guess's band finally holds the certainty.

Honest scope. The claim is not that needles ARE billiards. It is that one π is reachable two maximally different ways — exact-and-instant (a count) and converging-into-a-corridor (an estimate that never equals it) — and the agreement is what you watch. The spell is an integer (===, never moves); the stammer is an estimate with visible error. The latch fires on "the corridor contains π," never on "the caret equals π." That asymmetry is the whole point.
