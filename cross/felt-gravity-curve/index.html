what this proves, and what it doesn't

  • One law, two readings. The flyer's SOLVED equilibrium lean (read from the star-flyer's own bisection solver) is identically the felt-tilt of its own orbit: tan θ = ω²R/g IS φ = atan(Fr) to < 2.0e-11. The rotor's pin (read from the rotor's own holds predicate) fires exactly when that felt-tilt reaches the friction angle atan(1/μ) = 65.772255° — gap 0.0.
  • The collapse is honest — no warp. Both benches measured the same gravity g = 9.81 (the module asserts F.G === R.G at load), so the two beads sit on one arc because the law is one law — not because a fudge factor forced them. Each bead sits at its OWN Fr: the rides share a LAW, not an ω.
  • It is not a tautology — two negative controls bite. Rigid spokes lock the flyer's arms (θ ≡ 0 for every ω) so its bead peels to the x-axis while the real felt-tilt climbs. Frictionless wall kills the friction reserve (μ = 0) so the rider never pins and ω_c → +∞ (the μ-tick slides to 90°). At ω=0 every reading agrees on 0 (anti-vacuity).
  • Idealized. Point-mass chairs on massless inextensible chains; a point-mass rider on a rigid drum wall with dry Coulomb friction (single μ). The equilibrium lean and the pin threshold are exact; only the chairs' swing-in and the rider's sink are eased for the eye.
