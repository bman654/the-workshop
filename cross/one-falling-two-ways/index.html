One Falling, Two Ways

a cabin's fall and a jet's leave ride one √(2gh)

One brass height dial, the head h. Turn it and two things that never met move on the same law: a sealed cabin is released from h metres and reaches the brake zone at the entry speed v = √(2gh) — the deeper you fell, the faster you arrive; beside it a tank filled to head h leaks a jet from a base hole at v = √(2gh) — Torricelli's law, area-independent, the deeper the water the faster it leaves. One speed of arrival, one speed of leaving, the same √-groove. Down the middle the gold curve, and a warm cabin rider and a cool jet rider that overlap into one gold marker — the lock latches when they coincide to < 1e-9.

Honest scope. The two benches were measured with slightly different g (the drop-tower's 9.81 vs the water-clock's standard 9.80665). We hold the shared groove at the cabin's g and warp the tank's head by the exact factor gDT/gWC so the two laws sit on ONE curve to 4.4e-16. The un-warped 1.7076e-4 gap is not hidden — it is itself a proof: it equals EXACTLY 1 − √(gWC/gDT) (the teeth Leg 3 pins), so the collapse is engineered, not assumed. Flip a neg-control and the matching rider peels off the groove.

what this proves, and what it doesn't

  • The collapse is real and exact. The cabin's brake-entry speed (read from the drop-tower's own integrator) and the tank's Torricelli jet (recovered from the water-clock's own outflow ODE) are the SAME function of head. On the warped groove they agree to < 4.4e-16 across the whole sweep — both are √(2gh). The two cores are imported live and the two recovery adapters share no code.
  • The two g's differ — and that gap is itself a proof. The benches were measured at g_DT = 9.81 and g_WC = 9.80665. Hold the groove at the cabin's g and warp the tank's head by the exact factor g_DT/g_WC and they coincide. Remove the warp and the jet sits a fixed 1.7076e-4 (relative) below the cabin — and that fraction equals EXACTLY 1 − √(g_WC/g_DT), h-independent. The warp is load-bearing, not cosmetic.
  • It is not a tautology — two negative controls bite. Clip to cable and the cabin coasts at a flat speed: its rideRatio v/√h spreads 2.449× over the heads, so a √-collapse classifier provably FAILS it. Metronome bore swaps the orifice for the water-clock's own even-ticking bore: the surface drops at the flat WC.C = 0.02 m/s, whose rideRatio varies — it cannot ride the √ either. The real free-fall and true-jet rideRatios are the SAME constant √(2g) to machine-ε.
  • Idealized. Point masses, no air drag, an inviscid jet, an instantaneous release. The exact √(2gh) is the clean ideal both benches claim; a real cabin has drag and a real jet has a vena-contracta discharge coefficient — those soften the corners, they don't move the law.
