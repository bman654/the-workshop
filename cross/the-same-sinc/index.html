The slit's hard-edged far field is the INTENSITY |Ã(k)|² = sinc²(k·w), sinc(t)=sin(t)/t; its first dark fringe is where the AMPLITUDE sinc first crosses zero, at k₁ = π/w (radians). The sampled tone's amplitude envelope is the normalised |sinc(f·w)|, sinc(x)=sin(πx)/(πx); its first spectral null is at f₁ = 1/w (cycles). a cross · The Squeeze × The Sampling Theorem

Pinch one brass rectangle of half-width w and two benches that never met read its Fourier transform — a sinc — and each finds its first zero at the same reciprocal 1/w. A hard-edged slit fans its light into an intensity |Ã(k)|² = sinc²(k·w); its first dark fringe sits at k₁ = π/w. A sampled tone carries an amplitude envelope |sinc(f·w)|; its first spectral null sits at f₁ = 1/w. The slit speaks in radians, the window in cycles — divide the slit's null by a lone, surfaced ÷π and the two are the identical reciprocal. Below, on one shared u = 1/w axis, an amber light-squared fan and a teal envelope stroke drop their first notches on the same gold tick. Same zeros, different costumes.

Drag the pinch-handle on the rectangle's right edge (or the slider) and watch the rectangle's own half-width BECOME w. Pinch narrower → the rectangle shrinks, the amber light-squared fan and the teal envelope BOTH fatten in lockstep, and BOTH first-null notches slide outward pinned to the rising gold 1/w tick — a gold plumb drops through them when they coincide to <1e-9. The lone ÷π gate between the amber skin and the shared axis is the whole reconciliation: the slit's null is π/w in radians, divided by π to land on 1/w. Then flip the knife-switch to gauss: the slit's far field becomes a smooth bell, its amber notch vanishes, the plumb goes dark, and the cartouche drops to NO NULL — the teal window null stays. It is the rectangle's HARD edges, not the apparatus, that force 1/w.

what this proves, and what it doesn't

  • One rectangle, two costumes, one first-zero. The slit's first dark fringe (the first zero of the slit core's own amplitude sinc) is at k₁ = π/w; the sampled tone's first spectral null (the first zero of the sampling core's own normalised sinc) is at f₁ = 1/w. Divide the slit's radian null by a single surfaced ÷π and the two are the identical reciprocal, equal to < 4.5e-16 over the whole w sweep.
  • The π is surfaced, not smuggled. The arguments — π for the slit's sinc, 1 for the window's — are discovered by scanning each parent's own function for its first sign-change, never re-typed. The only factor between the slit's radian null and the shared rail is that one ÷π, drawn as a gold gate on the frame. There is no free knob to tune it away.
  • The side-lobes DON'T match — and that's the honesty. The amber skin is the slit's INTENSITY (light squared, a luminous fan with a dark first fringe); the teal skin is the bare amplitude ENVELOPE. Their lobe heights genuinely differ — we do NOT force them to coincide. What coincides is the zeros: same first null, same first null, on the same gold tick.
  • The neg-control breaks the LAW (touchable). Flip the knife-switch to gauss and the slit's far field exp(−2w²k²) never crosses zero — slitHasNull('gauss')===false — so there is NO amber null to coincide with; the plumb goes dark and the cartouche drops to NO NULL. The apparatus is unchanged; only the EDGES changed. It is the rectangle's HARD edges, not the widget, that force 1/w — and a vacuous always-"coincident" checker provably fails this leg.
  • Toy, exact zeros. Both nulls are exact first zeros of the parents' own sinc; only the rendered fan and stroke are eased for the eye. The window skin is the analytic envelope sinc(w·f), not a discrete FFT (the DTFT Dirichlet kernel has a slightly different first null and an N-dependence) — the honest continuous companion of the slit's continuous far field.
