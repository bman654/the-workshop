The Shape They Share

two physics, one number.

One brass dial. Pull it and watch two worlds that never met reshape in lockstep: a hanging chain on the left (it minimises gravitational energy) and a soap film between two rings on the right (it minimises area). They share no equations and no code — yet from the same geometry both report the same shape parameter a of the curve a·cosh(s/a), lit in gold down the middle. Pull far enough and the film gives up — it snaps to two flat discs — while the chain hangs on, unbothered. The agreement breaks on purpose, exactly where the geometry says it must.

Honest scope. The claim is not that a chain IS a soap film. It is that both problems are the curve a·cosh(s/a), and from the same rings/pins both report the same a — to machine precision — until the film's existence constraint runs out. Each stage also shows its own raw physical quantity (the chain's sag & span · the film's neck & ring radius) beside the shared gold a, so you watch two different worlds collapse onto one ruler rather than being told they are "the same."
