The Midway · the amusements district

A figure skater spins on one blade, arms flung wide with a weight in each hand — on a free, frictionless pivot, already turning when you arrive. No one pushes her. Pull her arms in and she speeds up — not because anyone added spin, but because the spin she already had had nowhere to go. The momentum L = I·ω is kept; making I small makes ω big. Let the arms back out and she slows to exactly where she began.

What you are watching

The whole story rides on one number: the rotor's moment of inertia I(r) = I₀ + 2m·r² — how hard it is to spin, set by how far the two hand-weights sit from the axis. On the free pivot nothing twists her from outside, so her angular momentum L = I·ω can't change. We pin it at the arms-out value L₀ = I(A)·ω_A, and from then on the spin is forced, not chosen: ω(r) = L₀ / I(r). Pull the arms in and I shrinks, so ω must rise — by exactly the inverse ratio, ω(B)/ω(A) = I(A)/I(B). That is the entire trick, and it is the same reason a diver tucks and a galaxy's core spins fast: she made herself small, and the world sped up.

The spin drawn in the rink is never eased or faked — the motion-blur fan's reach is the live arm-span and its blur-width is read straight off ω(r) = L₀/I(r). As the arms tuck the fan shrinks in reach and at the same instant widens into a whirring disc. Letting the arms back out is just as instructive: she slows to exactly the start — it's no ratchet, the law runs both ways.

Momentum is kept, but energy is not: the kinetic-energy bar rises as you pull in, by ΔKE = ½L₀²(1/I_B − 1/I_A) > 0. Nothing added that energy to the spin — it is the work your arms did, hauling the weights inward against the centrifugal demand m·ω(r)²·r at every radius. The self-test integrates that arm-work and confirms it equals the energy gained to one part in a billion. (Try it: the energy bar never falls for free.)

The neg-control — clamp to a motor

Flip CLAMP TO A MOTOR and an iron drive belt bolts onto the pivot, driving a fixed ω. Watch two things break at once. First, pulling the arms in now changes the spin not at all — the fan's blur-width is frozen, because the motor pins ω; the magic vanishes. Second, the once-pinned L bar swings free: with ω held and I shrinking, L = I·ω is no longer conserved — the motor is pouring angular momentum in and out. This is the proof by removal: take away the free pivot and the law dies, exactly inverting the free case (there ω varied and L held; here ω holds and L varies). One label rides the motor housing: “an external torque holds ω — so L is no longer yours to keep.”

Honesty

Two point masses m on massless rigid arms, a frictionless bearing, quasi-static retraction so L is exactly conserved at every instant. A real skater's distributed arm and torso mass means the literal ω-ratio differs — the law is exact for this point-mass model. The motion-blur fan persistence and the weight-trails are rendering choices and enter no tested number; ω, L, KE and I are all exact core values. ω is reported as illustrative turns/sec for feel.
