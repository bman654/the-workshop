The Golden Seed-Head

A sunflower lays each seed one fixed turn from the last. Turn the brass dial to set that angle. At the golden angle the head packs into two interleaved spiral families — a clockwise set and a counter-clockwise set — and if you count the arms in each you always get two consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Those are exactly two consecutive denominators of φ = [1;1,1,…], the slowest-converging fraction there is. Grow the head or sweep the dial and the counts click up the ladder in lock-step — never landing between. Turn to a tidy fraction p/q and the spirals collapse into q dead-straight spokes: you can feel why the flower refuses a fraction. Trace an arm with your finger to count it by hand.

The two arm-counts are found geometrically — each floret's nearest neighbour in space differs from it by a fixed index step, and those steps pile up at the parastichy numbers; the bench reports the two most populous. The ladder beside it is pure number theory — φ's continued fraction, the same core the Best Rational bench proves exact.
