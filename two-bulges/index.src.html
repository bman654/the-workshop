Why two bulges? (drag the REVEAL knob and read this)

The Moon's gravity is stronger on the near side of Earth than at the centre, and weaker on the far side. The part that is the same everywhere — the pull at Earth's centre — just makes the whole Earth fall freely toward the Moon; nothing on the surface feels it (you fall with it). Subtract that average pull and what is left is the tide: a pure stretch along the Earth–Moon line, pulling the water outward at both ends — near side toward the Moon, far side away from it. Two bulges. The leading size of each is 2GMR/d³: halve the distance and the tide grows eightfold; double it and the tide drops to an eighth. The near and far bulges are equal to leading order (the near one is a hair bigger). Pull REVEAL from 0 to 1 to watch the raw Moon-ward arrows turn into that pure two-ended stretch.
