Turn one knob. Above, the Spirograph pen sweeps a rosette whose petals number R / gcd(R, r). Below, the same R×r rectangle is cut by the largest square that fits, again and again — Euclid's anthyphairesis, made of squares — spiralling down to the smallest square that tiles both rods. That square is the gcd, and it sets the petal count. Two rooms, one integer — reached by code that never met.
