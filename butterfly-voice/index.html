A note's pitch IS the exact FFT bin its spectrum peaks in. Pick a note: the pitch that names it — semiToFreq(s), the same function that voices the Carillon's bells — predicts the integer bin c where the imported radix-2 FFT places its peak. Under coherent sampling the line lands in one bin: kmeasured = kpredicted = c, exactly.

The note — pick a pitch

Click a key on the strip (or use ◀ ▶ / the arrow keys). The picked key gilds; the waveform and the spectrum both update from the SAME synthesised buffer. Default: middle C (s=0).

The sampling — break it yourself
Coherent
Device-rate (48kHz)

In coherent mode we DERIVE the sample rate so exactly c whole cycles fit the window — the tone IS the DFT basis vector at bin c, so the match is geometry, not luck. Flip to device-rate and the cycles don't close: the line LEAKS across bins, the peak is only the NEAREST bin, and k·Δf recovers f to ±Δf/2, not exactly.

The window — N (power of two)
1024
4096
16384

Exactness is (f, N, fs) aligning, not "more samples." Every choice here is a power of two — the butterfly's radix-2 requirement. A non-power-of-two N makes the transform throw (self-test LEG D).
