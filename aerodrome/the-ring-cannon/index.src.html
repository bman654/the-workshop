A ring is not a lump of air

why one of them arrives and the other does not

Push a slug of air out of a round hole and the lip of the hole shears it. The shear rolls up, and what leaves is not the slug: it is a closed loop of vorticity — a doughnut of spin with circulation Γ around it. From that moment on nobody is pushing it. Every part of the loop simply moves at the velocity the rest of the loop induces, which is Helmholtz's theorem, and the sum of those velocities happens to point forwards. The ring carries itself.

A puff has none of that. It is momentum in a bag of air, and a turbulent bag grows in proportion to how far it has come. Momentum is fixed, volume goes like the cube of the distance, so the speed falls like the cube of the distance and the smoke in it is diluted by the same factor. It is not that the puff stops — it is that it takes forever and you cannot see it. Fire both. They leave with the same impulse. The panel prints where each of them is.

What the room actually computes

one line, and everything else falls out of it

Each ring is a closed filament of nodes. Every node moves at

u(p) = (Γ/4π)  ∮  dl × r / (|r|² + δ²)3/2

summed over every loop in the hall — its own included — plus the loop's mirror image in the back wall when it is close enough for that to matter. That is the entire dynamic. The leapfrog is not scripted; the ballooning at the wall is not scripted; the smoke rolling into the core is not scripted. They are all this integral.

The softening length δ is not the core radius, and getting that wrong is the classic way to build a ring that flies at the wrong speed. The regularised loop translates at (Γ/4πR)·(ln(8R/δ) − 1), while Kelvin's real thin-cored ring goes at (Γ/4πR)·(ln(8R/a) − ¼). Set the two equal and δ = a e−3/4 — a closed form, not a fit. The twin does not take that on faith: it bisects numerically for the δ that reproduces Kelvin, at four core thicknesses, and the answers walk in on e−3/4 at second order in a/R.

Two rings, and the one number that does not move

the dance, and the ruler laid across it

Fire a pair. The back ring finds itself inside the front one's flow, which pulls it narrower and speeds it up; the front one is pushed wider and slows. The narrow one shoots through, opens out, and the two change places. Then again. Each radius swings by forty per cent — and the hydrodynamic impulse, which for a ring is ρΓπR², does not move at all. So R1² + R2² is a constant of the dance, and the panel prints its drift in parts per million while you watch.

That the filament can leapfrog is not evidence that it leapfrogs right. So the twin runs a second model beside it that shares no code at all: coaxial circles, four numbers, velocities from the closed-form field of a circular vortex ring in complete elliptic integrals — no Biot–Savart, no nodes, no regularisation, no band limit. Over the length of this hall the two agree. Past that the rings thread each other closely enough that a thin-filament model has no business being believed, and the room says so rather than showing you a pretty lie.

The smoke is not decoration

sixty-five thousand tracers in the same field

The smoke you see is sixty-five thousand particles, each one moved on the GPU by the same kernel the filament obeys — the exact same nine lines of algebra, spliced from rings.mjs into the advection shader so the two cannot drift apart. Nothing about the smoke is animated; it is in the flow. That is why it rolls into the core, why the stray wisps left at the mouth get drawn along behind, and why a ring passing through another ring's smoke drags it inside out.

It is composited by absorption rather than by adding — a pixel's colour is scene·e−kD + lit·(1−e−kD) over accumulated optical depth D — which is the difference between smoke and plasma, and costs one line.
