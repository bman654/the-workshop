Pour NaOH into a flask of HCl drop by drop. The flask stays electrically neutral at every drop, so its pH(V) is the exact root of a quadratic — and it passes through 7.000000000 at the equivalence volume, where the moles of base finally match the moles of acid. Watch the flask: phenolphthalein stays colorless… then, a hair past true equivalence, the first faint pink holds. Open the stopcock and chase it by feel — the curve in the rail is only the shadow.

precision trial · find the unknown

A flask of HCl at an unknown concentration sits on the plate. Titrate until the pink holds, then Lock In the volume — the analyst back-solves your Ĉₐ from the recorded endpoint and grades it against the truth. Rinse & repeat for a fresh aliquot of the SAME unknown; the score rewards concordance (your trials agreeing within ±1 drop), not luck.

The two flags in the curve tell the honest story: the magenta flag at your endpoint (where the eye saw pink) sits a hair to the right of the cyan flag at true V_eq. That gap is the titration error — phenolphthalein turns at pH 8.2, not 7, so you always over-shoot by a sliver. At 0.1 M the gap is a fraction of one drop; dilute to 0.001 M and it opens to more than a drop. None of this is asserted by hand — the self-test pill proves every claim, and the flask's color is computed from the proven pH alone, so the picture can never disagree with the number.
