The Limiting Reagent

Load moles of two reactants onto the brass pans and run the reaction. It proceeds turn by turn until the first pan runs dry — that reactant is the limiting reagent, and the reaction stops there no matter how much of the other is left. The extent ξ = min(nᵢ/cᵢ) decides everything: which pan empties, the exact excess left over, and the product yield — all in exact whole-number fractions, never a rounded float. Pour the perfect ratio and every pan empties together at once: a dead-level beam, zero leftovers. The faint curve below is only the shadow — the draining pans are the thing.

The pans never weigh grams — they weigh moles of reaction turns. Each reactant can sustain only nᵢ/cᵢ turns before its pan empties; the reaction runs to the smallest of those, so the scarcest reactant limits it and the rest sit in excess. None of this is asserted by hand: the extent, the leftovers, the yield and the perfect-pour tie are all decided by exact BigInt-rational comparison — the self-test pill proves it, and the stamped pans read straight off the same numbers, so the picture can never disagree with the proof. The atoms in these pans are the same ones the Reaction Balancer orders and the Titration bench counts.
