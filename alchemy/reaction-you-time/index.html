The Reaction You Time

Set the flame and hit START — a brass flask of molecules winks out, and a clock-marker drops each time the flask halves. Pour a fuller flask: the ticks land at the SAME even spacing, just one more at the top — the half-life does not care how full you started. Throw the brass lever to SECOND order, where a decay needs two molecules to MEET, and each successive half-life DOUBLES, the ticks spreading wider. You read the reaction ORDER straight off the clock.

The clock is laid from the law, not from the dice: the renderer asks core.tickSpacings(order, A₀, k, n) for the successive half-lives and drops a ghost rung at each cumulative crossing time — it never decides a crossing itself. The live brass pip lands beside that ghost, near it but jittering, because the flask you watch is a discrete Monte-Carlo of individual molecules. One noisy pour can't trace the smooth curve — so the self-test proves the ensemble mean of many pours converges to the simulation's own mean-field within 4·SE (never on a single run), and that the mean-field → the continuous law as the timestep shrinks. The atoms winking out are the same ones the Titration bench pours drop by drop.
