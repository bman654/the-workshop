The Crystal Garden

A jar of water glass. Drop a grain of metal salt on the floor and a hollow mineral tube seeps upward through its own coloured membrane — leaning, swaying, splitting into a thicket. Each metal grows to its own habit. Nothing to solve. Pick a salt, place your seeds, and watch the garden grow — it keeps growing while you're here, and remembers the jar when you come back.

Pick a salt below, then click low in the jar to plant it. (Drop one near the surface and it barely grows — there's no water column above it to draw up.)
the jar remembers

Each salt cracks its silica gel a different colour and a different shape: cobalt climbs blue & spindly, iron knots rust-red, copper bushes low in green, nickel a jade middle, manganese fine & pink — the same osmotic upwelling that grew the old chemists' “silica gardens.” It proves nothing. It is only slow, and alive.
