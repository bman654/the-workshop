The Alchemy Lab

The estate's first chemistry wing. Each bench is a hanging balance on the laboratory wall — a law of matter you can feel as weight and level. The atoms the Cavern's physics drift takes apart are the ones you put back together here. Eight balances now hang lit and level, no cradle left waiting: the Reaction Balancer, Titration, the Limiting Reagent, Le Chatelier's Vise, the Aufbau Staircase — where you climb the n + ℓ ladder and watch the periodic table be born one electron at a time — the Galvanic Cell, where two metals make a brass needle swing to the difference between them, The Reaction You Time, where a flask of molecules winks out and a clock-marker drops each time it halves — even rungs for first order, spreading for second — and The Fractionating Column, where dragging the flame walks a separation staircase up the glass plates until a receiver fills with near-pure distillate.

Eight balances hang level; no cradle waits. Each bench keeps the workshop's promise — where it makes a claim, a built-in self-test proves it. The Reaction Balancer reads coefficients off the nullspace; Titration roots the equivalence pH from charge balance; the Limiting Reagent decides the scarcest pan from ξ = min(nᵢ/cᵢ) — all exact; Le Chatelier's Vise settles the extent where Q(ξ) ≡ K, then shoves it toward fewer molecules when you compress; the Aufbau Staircase climbs the n + ℓ ladder until the table is born exact — periods sealing at {2, 10, 18, 36}; the Galvanic Cell swings its needle to E°cell = E°cat − E°an, additive over every pair, flipping sign but not size when you swap, and dead at zero when both metals match; the Reaction You Time drops a clock-marker each time the flask halves — even rungs for first order (t½ = ln2/k, blind to how full you poured), spreading for second (each half-life doubling); and the Fractionating Column walks the stepped VLE staircase that equals Fenske's αN+1 to machine epsilon, collapsing dead flat at α = 1 where nothing separates.  ·  Back to the estate map
