the whole physics
  1. Lift. Take one slab off a random cell — unless the cell is in the lee of something upwind, where the wind never reaches.
  2. Carry. Walk it downwind. It sticks where it lands with a fixed chance — or at once, and always, if it reaches a lee.
  3. Slide. Anything steeper than 34° avalanches until it isn't.

Nothing in that knows what a dune is.

honest about the model

The ripples are drawn, not solved — a 10 cm ripple is a twentieth of a cell. The vertical is exaggerated (the slider says by how much). There is no wind speed here, only a direction and a rate. The clock comes from setting the model's measured flux, 4.50 m² a sweep, against a working erg's 60 m² a year; halve that and halve every year on the wall. Everything else is measured, and the bench measures it in front of you.

the race — speed against 1/height

Six ridges, six sizes, one wind. Each is spun up until it stops changing shape, then timed. If a dune's speed really is a flux divided by its height, these six points lie on one straight line through the origin.

the gate — where the sand is, against what crossed

One ridge. The amber curve is its speed times its own profile — measured from where the sand is. The blue is the sand counted through a gate at every station, by saltation and by avalanche both. Nothing connects the two measurements.
