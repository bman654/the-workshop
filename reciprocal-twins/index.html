The Reciprocal Twins

One brass slider. One ruler down the middle. Drag the period p and watch two worlds that never met move in lockstep: a laser's diffracted spots overhead and a plucked string's overtones below, every feature landing on the SAME integer tick. Both are the Fourier transform of a single period — so both are the comb {n/p}. Widen p, both combs squeeze toward center; narrow it, they fan out and the pitch climbs.

Honest scope. This shows the shared reciprocal-comb structure of two periodic systems. The acoustic period is taken as p = the fundamental's half-wavelength — the chosen convention that makes both draw at n/p. This is not a claim that a grating IS a string. Each band shows BOTH its raw physical quantity (sinθ in degrees · f in Hz) and the normalized reciprocal coordinate, so you watch two different worlds collapse onto one ladder — λ and the speed of sound both cancel.
