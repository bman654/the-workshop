The Jug

Fill a brass jug and blow across its mouth and it hums one low note — with no overtone ladder at all. The plug of air in the neck is a mass bobbing on the cavity's air-spring: a single lumped resonance. Pour water in to halve the air and the hum jumps a tritone (×√2); stretch the neck four-fold and it drops a clean octave. The lumped foil to The Stopped Pipe's distributed odd-harmonic series — there is nothing at 2f to draw.

THE JUG — the gold air-slug bobs on the cavity's air-spring; the pip on the right rail is the heard pitch

Drag the three bronze handles — raise the water (less air → higher), widen the throat (→ higher), lengthen the neck (→ lower). Click the mouth to flick it (one note, fading); press & hold to blow a steady hum. Blow harder (drag up while held) and it gets louder — never higher: the pip doesn't move.

Why a jug hums one note (and where the pitch comes from)

The plug of air in the neck is a mass, m = ρ·A·Leff (density × bore area × effective neck length). Push it inward and the trapped air in the body is squeezed, so the pressure rises and shoves back — an air-spring of stiffness k = ρc²A²/V. A mass on a spring rings at one frequency, fH = (1/2π)√(k/m) = (c/2π)√(A/(V·Leff)) — the ρ cancels. One lumped mode. There is nothing at 2f.

The three levers (each an exact interval): halve the air V→V/2 ⇒ ×√2 (a tritone, +600¢, since f∝1/√V); double the throat A→2A ⇒ the same ×√2 by a different lever (f∝√A); quadruple the neck Leff→4Leff ⇒ ×½, a clean octave down (f∝1/√Leff). The negative control: blow harder and the hum gets louder — never higher (the resonator is linear; the pip doesn't move). This is the lumped foil to The Stopped Pipe's distributed odd-harmonic ladder.

Honesty. The slug is shown at 1/72 speed — the ear hears it 72× faster. The drawn jug is a 2D depiction; V is a clean calibrated volume and the claim is fH given (A,V,Leff), not the jug's true 3D shape. Leff folds in the neck end-correction (the effective length runs a little past the bore). The audible ring is fd = fH√(1−1/4Q²), ≤0.3¢ below the fH the pip reads — the same note. The headless heard-claim is made by verify.sh on the SAME bandpass at five pinned geometries.

self-test —

The self-test proves it EXACT: across a 400-geometry sweep (2πfH)² = k/m to the bit (the pitch IS √(k/m), a mass on a spring) and fH is independent of the air density ρ; halving V and doubling A each give exactly ×√2 (+600.000¢), quadrupling Leff exactly ×½ (−1200.000¢); and a matrix-exp ODE render of the lumped resonator leaves only fH alive (the energy at 2f,3f sits at the far-skirt floor, three orders down from a harmonic ladder), with an 8× drive reproducing the waveform bit-for-bit — louder, never higher.
