The Beating Bench

Consonance is not a number you read off a chart — it is the place where the beating stops. A steady drone sounds; you drag one tone across it by hand. The two voices throb; drag toward a just ratio and the throb slows to a dead stop. The silent islands are the consonances themselves.

Drag the gold tone. The two glowing traces are the nearest pair of partials — one from the drone, one from the tone — and the needle reads their beat rate: the throb you HEAR is exactly |fhi − flo| of that pair. Slide onto a green island and the pair merges: the beat falls to 0 Hz and the sound goes glassy-still.

Why the islands are the consonances

The drone rings harmonics at f₀, 2f₀, 3f₀…; the dragged tone rings f₀r, 2f₀r, 3f₀r…. At a simple ratio p/q the drone's p-th partial and the tone's q-th partial land on the same frequency — the pair coincides and the beat is exactly zero. That is why the lit islands are precisely the classical consonances (unison·m3·M3·P4·P5·m6·M6·octave), while the complex just ratios (m2, M2, the tritone, m7, M7) never quite still — dissonance is the throb that won't die.

The honest test: jump to the comma-detuned third — a just major third (5/4) nudged by the syntonic comma 81/80, landing on 81/64. It looks a hair from M3, but its partials do not coincide, so it still throbs. You cannot fake stillness by snapping to the nearest semitone or by visual proximity — only the true ratio reaches silence.

self-test —

The self-test proves it EXACT: the needle (beatRate) equals |Δf| of the nearest partial pair to the bit; the beat is 0 Hz at every consonant island and nonzero at every complex ratio (and at the comma-detuned third — the load-bearing negative control); and the silence is a true minimum you slow into. The new beat physics is single-sourced to core.mjs; the tuning lattice (JUST_SET, cents) is single-sourced to pitch-core.mjs — both byte-twinned into this page, with the Node twin core.test.mjs proving char-for-char parity.
