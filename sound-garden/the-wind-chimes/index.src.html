The cord’s toll

A cord clamps whatever it touches, and drains each mode in proportion to how far that mode moves where the cord passes — Yn(ξ)². So there is one place a cord may touch a tube without killing its fundamental: the point where mode 1 stands still.

Press measure it: the page will strike a tube at 33 hanging positions, band-pass the fundamental out of the audio it just made, and fit the decay. Nothing is looked up.

Two things you can also just do. Drag the cord along a tube and hear twelve seconds fall to two. And tap a tube at its exact middle: the second partial vanishes, because 0.5 is a node of mode 2 as surely as 0.2242 is a node of mode 1 — where you hit decides the timbre, and where you hang decides the length. (The bending you see is honest in shape and a liar in size: it is drawn 140× slow and some thousands of times too large, or you would see nothing at all.)
