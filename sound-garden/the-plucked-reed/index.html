The Plucked Reed

Drag across the brass reed and let go to pluck it. A burst of raw hiss scatters at the spot you released — then, lap by lap, it self-organizes into a clean damped standing wave that rings at a pitch. The wave you watch is the buffer you hear. Now drag DECAY to the floor: the echo loop opens, the string dies, and the reed falls back into the noise it was made of.

Why a string is just hiss in an echo loop

The pluck fills a delay line of length N = round(SR/f) with a burst of seeded noise. Then every sample, the loop reads the line one lap back and feeds it forward through a tiny two-tap average: buf[i] = g·(b·buf[i−N] + (1−b)·buf[i−N−1]). Each lap is exactly N samples = one period, so the buffer's local shape is one period of the standing wave — the curve you watch is the buffer you hear.

The reveal: the loop keeps re-circulating the same hiss, and the two-tap average shaves a little off the highs each lap — so the jagged noise smooths, lap by lap, into a pitched wave whose period is N. Pull DECAY to zero and the loop stops feeding back: no echo, no string, just the one-shot hiss. The honesty: that two-tap filter has gain exactly 1 at DC and ≤1 at Nyquist — it can only remove the hiss's highs, never inject a tone. The pitch was carved from noise by the delay length, not added by a hidden oscillator.

self-test —

The self-test proves it EXACT: the autocorrelation period of the settled reed is N+(1−b) (within a few cents of SR/N) at two pitches; the fundamental's per-lap decay equals the feedback gain g; a high partial survives longer as b rises; with g=0 there is NO pitched tail (only hiss); and the loop filter's DC gain is exactly 1 with Nyquist ≤1. The DSP is single-sourced to core.mjs, byte-twinned into this page, with the Node twin core.test.mjs proving char-for-char parity and that the recurrence is re-typed nowhere.
