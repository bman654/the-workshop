Out of Tune

Voice each planet's orbital period as a held organ tone — Earth on home A, every other world a single pitch in the octave above. The solar system obeys Kepler's law to the decimal… and yet the chord it sings lands audibly out of tune. Turn the brass dial; let your ear judge.

Now sounding

turn the dial to sound a chord

The window's worlds are held as sustained organ-drones — only a held chord lets your ear catch the slow throb of a sour interval. The brass tuning rail along the dial's lower rim ticks the cents-off for each adjacent pair now sounding; the sourest real pair is Mercury–Venus, 37.55¢ off a major third, and Saturn–Uranus is 24.14¢ off the tritone.

self-test —

The planets do obey Kepler: a³² = period to within 0.10% for every real world (worst 0.0616%, Neptune). The sourness is real too: voicing each period folds it to a tone that misses the nearest just interval by 5.35–37.55¢ — never a round-off artifact. The control Concordia (a fictional exact-3:2 world) passes Kepler and snaps to 0¢, proving the test has teeth.
