The Tartini Bench

In 1714 Giuseppe Tartini heard a third tone sounding under two he was bowing — il terzo suono, a pitch no string played. Tune two brass tone-wheels through a slightly-bent horn and HEAR it bloom at f₂−f₁ — then flip the horn LINEAR and it vanishes. Tune it by ear.

Why a third tone appears

Two pure tones summed are x(t) = cos(2πf₁t) + cos(2πf₂t) — only f₁ and f₂ live in that spectrum. Push them through a slightly-bent horn, y = x + ε·x², and the quadratic term breeds components the input never carried. (cos A + cos B)² contains cos(A−B) — a real tone at f₂−f₁, plus a sum tone, two octaves and a DC shift. The third pitch is the input fed through the bend, nothing added by hand.

The reveal: slide f₂ up and the green phantom dives down through the fulcrum — it sits at the mirror position f₁−(f₂−f₁), so a wider interval breeds a lower difference tone. The negative control: flip the horn LINEAR (ε=0, y=x) and the difference marker goes dead grey — the bend is gone, so there is nothing to breed. The bloom is the bend.

self-test —

The self-test proves it EXACT: diffToneFreq is |f₂−f₁| to the bit, the difference bin of the measured horn output is ε/2 to <1e−9, the whole x² expansion (sum ε/2, octaves ε/4, DC ε) matches, and on the LINEAR path every bred bin is a true 0. The physics is single-sourced to core.mjs, byte-twinned into this page, with the Node twin core.test.mjs proving char-for-char parity. The hero default (220·275 → 55 Hz) is math-confirmed only — 55 Hz is below the Audio Lens's 60 Hz floor, so the heard-headless claim is made by verify.sh on the SAME horn an octave up (2600·2750 → a 150 Hz difference tone the lens can find).
