The Spiral Ear

a note · and the one place it lands

Thirty-five millimetres of membrane, wound two and a half times, with water on both faces. Play something. The place that answers is the whole of hearing: every note you can name is somewhere on this ribbon, and the octave — anywhere you put it — is three and a half millimetres wide.

where it lands

the tone—
peak, from the stapes—
the place that resonates at it—
so the peak stands basal by—
it takes this long to get there—
…which is this many of its own cycles—
an octave, measured—

the cliff

an octave below this place—
an octave above this place—
and past the peak, always—

Beyond its own place the membrane goes mass‑controlled, the wavenumber turns real, and the wave stops travelling and simply dies — at √(2ρ/HM), which has no frequency in it at all. That one‑sided cliff is why a loud low note drowns a quiet high one and never the other way round.
