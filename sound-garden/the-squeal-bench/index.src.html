The Squeal Bench

A violin's sing, a door's creak, and the ground's quake are the same motion: something dragged, gripping and letting go. Drag the bow across the fibre — slow, hard, light, fast — and hear one gesture morph through all three. No buttons switch between them; you just move differently.

Drag left–right across the fibre (mouse or finger). A still finger holds the fibre under tension; drag fast & light to sing, slow & hard to creak, stiff & barely-moving to quake.

Under the fibre is a tiny weight on a spring, and the bow is a moving belt. Friction between them is velocity-weakening: it grips hardest when the two aren't sliding (static) and lets go weaker once they are (kinetic). So the bow drags the weight along, the spring stretches, the grip loses — snap — the weight flies back and the belt catches it again. Stick, load, slip, repeat. That single loop is a relaxation oscillator.

Slip a hundred times a second and the snaps fuse into a pitched sawtooth — a bowed string's sing (that is real Helmholtz motion, the physics of every violin). Press hard and drag slow and each slip is rare and huge — a low creak. Stiffen the spring and barely move and the releases come apart into a sparse train you can count — a quake. Same law, one dial-family; the word above is read from the measured slip-rate and let-go size, not from any switch. Lift the bow and it goes silent, because the sound was only ever the drag.
