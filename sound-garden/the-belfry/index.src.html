The Belfry

six bells · rung full circle

Nobody who rings ever sees this. A tower bell turns through very nearly a whole revolution and stands mouth up at each end, and it sounds when the clapper inside it catches up. Everything here is that, integrated as you watch.

one stroke behind — try it

Lean on the traced bell's rope for one stroke. Watch the strip: the blow it is already falling towards does not move. The one after it moves by a tenth of a second.

 

the note that is not there

A bell's nominal, superquint and octave nominal stand as 2 : 3 : 4, and the ear supplies the missing 1 — so the note you name a bell by is a pitch that is not in the sound (Schouten & 't Hart, 1965). Take away everything at or below it and the note stays where it was.

Turned off, this is measured, not asserted: point a pitch detector at what is left and it says G4, the nominal — 392, 590, 788 Hz and nothing below. It has no ear to supply the 1 with. You do.

measure this bell

Sweeps the traced bell over four hundred-fold in drop height, one full integration per point, and fits the fall time against ln(ε − ε*). Two or three seconds.
