The Endless Staircase

Turn the brass dial and a chord climbs — higher, and higher, and higher. Yet every full turn it lands exactly home, no higher than where it started. Nine sine partials sit an octave apart under a fixed bell of brightness; as they rise they slide up through the bell — a new faint one waking at the bottom as the top one dies. Then flip the envelope to LADDER and hear the trick break: the tone just climbs out and never returns.

Why it climbs forever yet never leaves

The tone is nine sine partials stacked an octave apart: fk = f₀·2(k + θ/12), where the dial reading θ is in semitones. A fixed Gaussian bell in log-frequency, pinned at middle C, sets each partial's loudness — the band of light you SEE is the law you HEAR. As θ climbs, every partial slides up through the stationary bell; a partial that climbs out the top is reborn at the bottom, so the chord reads its phase as frac(θ/12) and is genuinely periodic: the set at θ and at θ+12 is identical to the bit.

The illusion: the dial counter climbs without bound, but the chord folds home every octave — the spectral centroid never leaves a bounded band and resets exactly each turn. The honest test: flip to LADDER — the SAME nine partials, but a FLAT envelope and NO recycling. Now the centroid climbs strictly upward, exactly one octave per octave-shift, and EXITS the band. The loop was born of the fixed bell and the wrap, not the spacing.

self-test —

The self-test proves it EXACT: (A) at every phase the partial set at θ+12 equals the set at θ to the bit; (B) the illusion's centroid stays in a derived band and returns exactly to its start each octave; (C) the flat LADDER centroid climbs +1 octave per octave and exits the band; (D) the chord always carries ≥3 audible partials and never spikes, so it cannot clip.
