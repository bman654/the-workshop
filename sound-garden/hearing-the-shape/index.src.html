Can one hear the shape of a drum?

Two drumheads. Not the same shape — look at them. Strike them and they answer with exactly the same set of notes: fourteen of them here, agreeing to fifteen digits. Nobody chose these two outlines; the room found them by cutting seven half-squares and trying all 318 ways of gluing them together.

None of it is stored. Each panel below runs in this page, on this machine, when you open the drawer — the shapes are re-enumerated, the eigenvalues re-solved, and the pair re-found.

1 · the ladder, to every digit there is

The Dirichlet eigenvalues of the two drums, from two completely separate meshes with different connectivity. λ is what the solver returns; f is 110·√(λ/λ₁), so the only number chosen anywhere in this room is that the first note is an A.

2 · the solver, where the answer is known in closed form

Before believing a hard shape, check an easy one. A unit square's eigenvalues are π²(m²+n²) exactly; a half-square's are the same list with m > n. Both should converge like h², and the ratio between successive mesh halvings should be 4.

3 · against a published number the code was never given

λ₁ for these drums is a standard benchmark, computed to twelve digits by Driscoll (1997) for triangles with legs of length two. Ours have legs of length one and λ goes as 1/L², so the number to walk in on is four times his: 10.15177600. Nothing in this repo was fitted to it. The convergence is slower than h² and that is a prediction too: each drum has two 270° corners, every eigenfunction has an r^(2/3) singularity there, and that caps the rate at h^(4/3) — a ratio of 2.52, not 4.

4 · the search that found them

Every shape you can build from seven half-squares, edge to edge: 318 of them, and the count 1, 3, 4, 14, 30, 107, 318 is a published sequence (OEIS A006074) that nothing here was fitted to. Each row below is one shape's first eight eigenvalues, drawn as ticks and sorted by the first. 50,403 pairs get compared. Watch for the one row that has a twin.

5 · the ear — measured off this page's own output

Arithmetic agreeing with arithmetic is cheap. This puts an AnalyserNode on the signal that actually reaches your speakers, strikes drum I alone, holds the peak of its spectrum, then does the same for drum II, and draws both. Two lines, one shape.

6 · what a spectrum leaves out

Counting the notes cannot separate the twins. Neither can Weyl's law, which says the number of notes below a pitch depends only on the area and the perimeter — and these two have the same area and the same perimeter, 6 + 3√2. But the pictures of the notes are not the same. Count the pieces each mode's nodal lines cut its own drumhead into and the drums part company at the fourth note.
