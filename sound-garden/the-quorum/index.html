The Quorum

Synchrony is not a number you read off a chart — it is the instant a smear of clicks SNAPS into one pulse. Sixteen clocks tick at their own speeds, deaf to one another. Twist the one brass knob and they begin to hear each other — the hailstorm pulls itself into a single heartbeat.

Why the hailstorm becomes a heartbeat

Each clock advances at its own natural speed ωi; the knob adds a pull toward everyone else — θi ← θi + (ωi + (K/N)·Σ sin(θj−θi))·dt. With K small the pull loses to the spread of speeds and the phases smear round the circle: the order r = |&frac1n;Σeiθ| sits on the incoherent floor ≈1/√N. Twist K up and the pull wins — the clocks lock into one pack, r climbs toward 1, and every tick lands at once.

The honest test: flip the clocks deaf (the coupling muted, the ring still spinning) and turn the knob all the way up — nothing locks; r stays on the floor. Park the knob at K = 0 — same. The lock is the listening, not the motion. You cannot fake a quorum by spinning faster.

self-test —

The self-test proves it EXACT: steady-state order r rises as the coupling climbs (a measured K-ladder), sits on the 1/√N floor with no coupling (at two ring sizes — a law, not a point), never leaves the floor at K=0, and stays flat when the clocks are deaf even at full K — the load-bearing controls. The physics is single-sourced to core.mjs, byte-twinned into this page, with the Node twin core.test.mjs proving char-for-char parity.
