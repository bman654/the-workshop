free-drag (Hann)

Sample a signal at rate fs and every frequency below the Nyquist line fs/2 is carried exactly. Whittaker–Shannon threads a sinc through the samples and recovers the band-limited source to a residual of ~5e-14 — the live max |error| from the self-test, not the bare word "perfect." (The finite-window plain sum caps near 1e0; the periodic Dirichlet kernel removes that truncation — that is why it's the proof.)

Above the line, it's a masquerade. An undersampled cosine and its fold produce the byte-for-byte identical sample vector (equal to machine-ε, in fact strictly ===), so the samples cannot tell them apart. The reconstruction provably does not rebuild the source — it rebuilds the alias, exactly. Run the Butterfly's imported FFT and watch the spectral line land at the wrong bin.

The control — fs is load-bearing

Drag fs down. The gold comb thins; the reconstruction (green) lies on the true source (teal) until fs crosses 2·fmax — then green peels off teal and snaps to the ghost (red). The comb never moves: same samples, different signal. That snap is the exhibit.

Coherent ↔ free-drag

Presets are coherent (f·N/fs ∈ ℤ ⇒ one clean spectral stem; N=256). Free-drag breaks coherence, so the spectrum is computed with a light Hann window to tame leakage; the readout NAMES the active mode so the picture never lies. (Display windowing is separate from the byte-exact claim — that leg owns the integer-cycle LUT.)
