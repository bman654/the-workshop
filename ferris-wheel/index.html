What you are operating

This is not a chart of a Ferris-wheel ride — it is one, seen side-on. A single gondola rides a wheel of radius r turning at rate ω. The rider moves on a vertical circle, so the net force on them must always point inward, toward the axle, with magnitude mω²r. Only two forces act: gravity m·g straight down, and the seat’s push N — the scale reading. Resolve them along the circle and the whole story is one line: N(θ) = m(g + ω²r·cosθ), with θ measured from the bottom. At the bottom (θ=0) the seat must push hardest: N = m(g+ω²r). At the crest (θ=π) it pushes least: N = m(g−ω²r). The needle is that value, live, as the gondola goes around.

The float — and going past it

Wind the spin up and the crest reading falls. At exactly ω₀ = √(g/r) the crest goes weightless — Ntop = m(g−ω₀²r) = 0. That is the same true 0 g The Drop Tower stages by falling, here staged once per revolution, at the very top. And it carries no mass: an adult and a child float at the same ω₀ — slide the mass knob at the float and the crest stays pinned at 0. Push past ω₀ and the formula goes negative: a seat-pan can’t pull you down, so a negative reading means you’d lift OFF the pan and the lap-bar must hold you to the wheel. We do not clamp it at 0 — the negative number is the lap-bar’s downward pull, |Ntop| = m(ω²r−g). No other ride on the Midway sails past the float into restraint.

The parked-wheel control

Wind the spin back to ω = 0 and the wheel parks. Now ω²r = 0, so N(θ) = m·g for every θ — a flat reading all the way around, no swing. That is the neg-control you can feel: spin and the needle breathes; park it and the needle stands still at full weight. The self-test proves a flat-scale instrument (one that always reads m·g) disagrees with the real needle at every spinning seat — and agrees only on the parked wheel, where it is honestly right.

Honesty

This is the steady-state apparent weight at constant ω — we are not modelling the wheel spinning up, nor the gondola’s own pendulum sway (a real gondola also rocks; here we read the steady radial support the seat gives). The rider is a point mass; the wheel is rigid; g = 9.81 m/s²; the radius is r = 9 m, so the float lands at ω₀ = √(9.81/9) ≈ 1.044 rad/s (about 10 s a turn). Every value the eye sees — the needle angle, the live N legend, the lap-bar load — is N(θ,ω,m,r) from the one physics core.

The sister ride is The Drop Tower — there the whole room falls with you and the scale reads true 0 g for the length of the fall; here the wheel carries you around and the scale reads that same float at the crest — then past it, into the lap-bar.
