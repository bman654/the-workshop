The Next Word

You are at the table, betting against the model's own loaded die. A sentence stem appears; six candidate next-tokens lie on the felt — but the odds are hidden until you bet. Stake your chip on the word you think comes next, then the model reveals its true softmax distribution and rolls a die whose faces are literally sized by the probabilities. The word that lands costs −log₂ p bits of surprise — and your decoder, the greedy decoder (always the mode), and the uniform decoder each pay under their own bet. Low total bits = you read the die. Turn the temperature to make the die rounder or spikier — it's the same softmax the Temperature Dial turns.

The temperature knob

Warps the same softmax the Temperature Dial bench checks. Cold → the die spikes to one fat face (greedy). Hot → it rounds toward six equal slices (pure noise). Stake more chips on your read to widen the gap when you are right — and the loss when you are wrong.

The three decoders

Each pays −log₂ q on the word that landed, under its own bet: YOU stake-weight your guess, GREEDY puts all its chips on the mode, UNIFORM spreads evenly. Lower total bits is better — it means your decoder saw what the model would.

Greedy is dead-certain on the mode — cheap when it's right, dear when it misses; uniform never commits. You win by reading the die's geometry, not by luck.
