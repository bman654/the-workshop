The Escapement

A swinging pendulum meters out even beats; a toothed wheel releases one tooth per beat and an hour-hand notches them into hours — the engine that keeps time when the sun is gone. On the left, the sundial it beats against: the real sun wanders ±16 minutes over the year. In IDEAL mode the swing is isochronous — widen it and the beat is unchanged, the steady clock the small-angle law promises. Flip to REAL and a wide swing runs slow: a ghost-hand keeps the ideal time while the live hand lags, the gap widening into lost minutes. Both the bob and the count flow from one elliptic equation, so the picture cannot lie. Wind it and watch.

what this proves

The claim. A small-angle pendulum's period T = 2π√(L/G) carries no amplitude — that absence IS isochronism: widen the swing and the beat is unchanged, so the released-tooth rate is amplitude-blind (self-tested to <1e-9). The rod is pinned so this beat is exactly 2.000000 s. But the real finite-amplitude period is the elliptic integral T(θ₀) = 4√(L/G)·K(sin θ₀⁄2), strictly increasing in θ₀ — a wide swing runs slow, and the gap against the ideal grows into the lost minutes the readout shows. The visible bob is the closed-form elliptic solution, so the swing you watch and the teeth it counts share one authority. Every line is read from the equations, self-tested in the pill.

What it does NOT claim. A frictionless point-mass planar pendulum with a perfect escapement that neither drives nor damps — no mainspring, air drag, or temperature. The power series in θ₀ shown for contrast is only a witness (honest to ~45°, wrong by hand at 90°); the authority is the elliptic integral. The drift is read from the continuous phase gap, never the floored tooth-count difference. The knob is capped at 90°, clear of the swing-to-the-top singularity.
