Horology · the sun's wandering noon

The Analemma

Photograph the sun at clock-noon every day for a year and it never lands twice in the same place — it draws a slow figure-8 on the sky. That loop is the sum of two clocks pulled apart: the tilt of the axis (which alone draws a symmetric 8) and the stretch of the orbit (which alone draws a lopsided oval). Turn each knob to dial its cause from zero to real and watch the loop come apart into its two parents — kill both and the year collapses to a single dot.

what this proves

The claim. The analemma is the exact sum of two independent causes. This bench's sun is the same proven sun The Honest Sundial keeps — at the real settings (e = 0.0167, ε = 23.44°) every value here equals the dial's equation of time and declination to the thirteenth decimal. The self-test splits the loop: orbit-only term + tilt-only term ≡ the whole, day for day; the tilt alone makes a symmetric 8, the orbit alone a lopsided oval; and killing both causes leaves one fixed point — no loop. Every number on the wall is read from the equation, not drawn by hand.

What it does NOT claim. This is the low-precision almanac sun (the dial's own ~0.01° series), a fixed 2026 calendar, longitude 0 / UTC noon, mean obliquity. The eccentricity and tilt knobs scale the two physical causes faithfully but are a model, not an ephemeris — we show the SHAPE the two clocks make, not arc-second astrometry. The horizontal axis is stretched relative to the vertical (stated on the wall) so a ±16-minute swing reads against a ±23° one.

“The clock keeps an even hour the sun never does. Their quarrel, stamped night by night for a year, is this figure-8 — and it is exactly the tilt of the world plus the stretch of its road, and nothing else.”

The loop you watch is the proven equation; its shape is the consequence, self-tested in the pill, not asserted.
