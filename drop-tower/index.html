What you are operating

This is not a chart of a drop tower — it is one, seen in cutaway, and the camera rides inside the cabin. When you release, the room does not move on screen; instead the world — the mast struts, the ground markers — streaks up past the windows. That camera choice is the argument: you and the room are one falling frame. A loose coin that was resting on the floor lifts off and hangs in mid-air for the whole fall, because it and the floor obey the same gravity. Your arm and hair go slack; the dust suspends.

The brass scale on the cabin wall reads the apparent weight — the force the seat must push with to drag the rider along: N = m(g + a), where a is the cabin's own acceleration. At rest a=0 and it reads your full weight. In free fall a = −g, so N = 0 exactly — the needle pegs to zero and the 0 g lamp lights. At the brake a > 0 and it spikes to the crush.

The two knobs — both spatial, both honest

Raising the drop height visibly lifts the car higher up the mast (more stored fall). Shortening the brake band resizes the coral zone painted on the lower mast — and that band is exactly d metres tall, so you see the distance the brake gets to kill your speed. Read the trade off the brass plates before you ride: free fall from h arrives at v² = 2gh, and the brake must spend that over d, so the crush is the exact, monotone line peak g = h/d + 1. Re-ride with a harder brake and the live needle blows past the ghost mark of your last run.

Honesty

Idealized free fall: point masses, no air drag, an instantaneous cable release and an instant, constant-deceleration brake. The exact 0 g is claimed precisely for ideal free fall (a = −g ⇒ N = 0); the slow-motion toggle dilates only the animation clock, never the physics (the core samples positions, not wall-time, so the dwell is honest). The self-test includes a load-bearing negative control: a scale that reports full weight through the fall fails.
