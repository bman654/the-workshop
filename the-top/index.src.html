A fast wheel hangs from a rope by a gimbal. Its spin gives it momentum |L| = I·ω along the axle; gravity makes a torque τ = mgr·sinθ that is always horizontal and ⊥ to the axle. Since dL/dt = τ and a torque ⊥ L can only turn its heading, the axle does not fall — it orbits the rope at Ω = mgr/(I·ω). The sinθ cancels, so the lap time is the same at any lean, and spinning twice as fast makes it precess half as fast. Bleed the spin and L vanishes — now gravity finally topples it.
The Midway · the amusements district

A bicycle wheel hangs from a rope by one end of its axle. Held still it droops, axle nose-diving, dead weight on a string. But flick the rim — give it a hard fast spin — and something impossible happens: the axle lifts to horizontal and refuses to fall. Instead the whole wheel begins to orbit the rope, slowly, in a flat circle. Gravity is pulling exactly as hard as before; it simply can no longer make the wheel fall — only turn. That slow turn is precession, and its speed is set by one inverse law: Ω = mgr / (I·ω).

What you are watching

Two clocks run at once. The fast one is the wheel's own spin ω — the spokes blur into a translucent whirring disc, drawn straight from the live core ω, never an eased fake. The slow one is the whole wheel's orbit around the rope, the precession Ω. The axle tip paints a ring on the floor grid; a lap counter on the rail times it.

Why doesn't it fall? Its fast spin gives it an angular momentum L = I·ω pointing along the axle. Gravity makes a torque τ = mgr·sinθ about the gimbal — but that torque is always horizontal and perpendicular to the axle. Newton's rotational law dL/dt = τ then says the torque adds a tiny dL = τ·dt at the tip of L — and because that nudge is ⊥ to L, it changes L's heading but not its length. The axle can only swing sideways. Hit SHOW THE KINK to watch a single step: L turns, τ·L stays a flat green zero, |L| never budges. That right angle is the whole trick.

Two laws fall straight out, and you can feel both. Spin the wheel faster and the orbit crawls: Ω = mgr/(I·ω) is an inverse law, so halving ω doubles the lap time — the rail's product plate holds Ω·ω = mgr/I dead constant as you scrub. And the lean knob teaches lean-independence: tilt the axle more and the ring just gets wider, at exactly the same lap time, because the sinθ in the torque cancels the sinθ in the geometry — the cancellation is exact, not approximate.

The neg-control — bleed the spin

Hit BRAKE and watch the magic come apart. As ω falls the orbit accelerates (the inverse law again, the other way), the axle starts to sag into a real nutation wobble, and then — with the spin momentum gone — gravity finally does what your intuition demanded all along: the wheel topples straight down and dangles dead. There was never anything holding it up but the largeness of L. RE-SPIN to revive it: fully reversible, no ratchet. The lesson by removal — precession existed only because L was large.

Honesty

This is the fast-top approximation: |L| is treated as fixed along a steady axle, giving a clean steady precession. Real tops also nutate (the axle nods up and down a little as it precesses), and |L| is not exactly along the axle. This shows the leading-order precession the gyroscope is famous for. The self-test does not fake |L| conservation to machine-ε: it integrates the honest dL = τ·dt step around a full lap and proves the |L| drift is bounded by a derived fast-top error ∝ Ω/ω, is genuinely nonzero (a real approximation), and shrinks as ω grows. The whirring-disc blur and the floor-ring trail are rendering choices and enter no tested number; ω, Ω, L, τ are all exact core values.

Three faces of one invariant. Angular momentum L is conserved in three different theatres of the estate: on The Equal-Area Sweep it is conserved — orbital (a planet sweeps equal areas because nothing twists it about the Sun); on The Spinning Chair its magnitude |L|=Iω is conserved — rigid-body (a skater shrinks I and ω must rise); and here its heading is steered by torque — gravity cannot change |L|, only swing it sideways. L conserved orbital · |L| conserved rigid-body · L's heading steered by torque here.
It stands up on its head. The lawful wheel here can only be steered by gravity, never toppled. Its twin does the opposite — flick a squat little top and friction torques its axis right over: the centre of mass climbs against gravity until the thing spins bolt upright on its stem.
