The oldest camera: a dark room with a single pinhole. Rays from the scene outside cross at the hole and paint an inverted, left–right reversed image on the back wall. No lens — just geometry. Magnification m = v/u. Drag the hole size and find the sweet spot: too big blurs by geometry, too small blurs by diffraction. The optimum is the classic d opt = √(2.44·λ·v).

Geometric blur grows with the hole: Bgeo = d·(u+v)/u. Diffraction grows as it shrinks: Bdiff = 2.44·λ·v/d. Their quadrature sum has one minimum — the marked optimum. The self-test proves m, the image height, and that the analytic dopt equals the numeric minimiser to machine precision.
