The Coastline Rule

how long is a coastline? — measuring roughness

the log–log proof · slope = D

Ask how long a coastline is and you get a paradox: measure it with a shorter ruler and you find more length, without limit. The honest answer isn't a length — it's a dimension. Cover a shape with boxes of side ε, count how many it touches (N(ε)), shrink the boxes, and watch how fast the count grows: D = log N(ε) / log(1/ε). A smooth curve gives D = 1, a filled region D = 2 — and a fractal lands in between. This bench doesn't draw the dimension; it measures it, and proves the measurement against the known answer.
