The Carry Cascade

Addition is a chain of permissions. Add two numbers column by column and a carry topples leftward — but only as far as an unbroken run of columns will pass it through. Tap a column and walk the avalanche yourself; hit Add and watch the wave live or die the instant a column says no pass. The worst case sweeps the whole row — that O(n) ripple is the slow part of every adder. Flip on look-ahead and the same sum settles in one flash: every column's verdict is read in parallel, the gold chain lights at once.

The Two Addends

Tap a digit in row A or B to raise it (wraps round). Tap a sum column to add just that one column and launch a single topple — you are the carry, hand-walking the cascade. Or press Add for the whole wave.

Base

Changes the glyphs and the overflow threshold — the choreography is the same. A carry is born when a column reaches the base.

Set up a cascade

Worst case = every column at base−1, plus one — the carry sweeps the entire row, depth n. No avalanche = a lone carry that dies at the next column, depth 1. The difference is the lesson: the avalanche needs the unbroken propagate chain, not mere addition.

Look-ahead

Resolve every carry in one shot
Each column's flag — g generate, p propagate, k kill — doesn't depend on the carry coming in. So press Add with this ON and there is no ripple: the flags are already lit, and one flash resolves them all. An unbroken propagate chain lights as one continuous gold ribbon.

Motion

Generate / settled — the column made or absorbed the carry.
Propagate — it passes a carry straight through.
Overflow — the column hit the base; a carry is born and rides left.
This is the adder in every CPU. Ripple-carry is the simplest; the chain of permissions is its slow part, so real hardware computes every column's generate / propagate flag in parallel and resolves the carries in one shot — carry-lookahead. You just felt the trade: fewer steps, more wires.
