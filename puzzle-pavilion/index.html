🧩 The Puzzle Pavilion

A gallery of provable puzzles where the line you draw is the whole game. The first four are generated deduction puzzles — every board proven unique and solvable by pure logic, never a guess. The fifth turns the verb outward: five in flower now — The Bridge House drags spans until a numbered archipelago fuses into one network; The Cross-Sums fills 1–9 so every run sums to its clue; The Pearl Loop draws one closed loop the scattered pearls pin to a single shape; The Switchyard rotates pipe tiles until the whole lattice drinks from one source; and The District Line carves a frozen electorate into districts where the lines alone decide who wins.

The Bridge House
Hashiwokakero · the estate's first "connect" verb

A dark archipelago of numbered islands on a sea. Drag from one island to an aligned neighbour to lay a span — drag again for a double, once more to clear. Win when every island carries exactly its number and the whole graph is one connected network. Watch separate islands flood green as they fuse into a single continent — kin to the Giant Component, but a logic puzzle you win.

The Cross-Sums
Kakuro · the estate's first "arithmetic crossword"

A grid of runs, each labelled with a target sum; tap a white cell and a digit 1–9 so every run hits its clue with no digit repeated within a run. Watch each run flush green as it tallies, the conflicts flare red, and win when every run sums true. The whole board has a single finishing — and a pure-deduction solver reaches it by the combinatorics of which digit-sets can make each sum, never a guess.

The Pearl Loop
Masyu · the estate's first "draw one closed loop"

Black and white pearls scattered on a grid pin exactly one closed loop — and you find it by dragging. Lay the loop segment by segment: it must turn at each black pearl (straight one cell on both arms) and run straight through each white (turning at a shoulder). Illegal segments flush red; the instant the single loop honours every pearl it snaps closed. A logic solver threads it from the forced turns outward, never a guess.

The Switchyard
Netwalk · the estate's first "rotate" verb

A dark lattice of pipe tiles with one glowing source. Rotate each tile a quarter-turn — by click or arrow-key — until the whole network fuses and every arm drinks from the source. There is no losing state: an un-powered tile is only half-lit in slate, waiting its turn. The instant the source reaches every tile the lattice settles into gold. One solved orientation, reachable by pure logic — and every single arm is load-bearing.

The District Line
Redistricting · the estate's first CIVIC puzzle / the "carve" verb

The votes are frozen; only the lines move. Carve a fixed map of red and blue voters into equal, contiguous districts — and every wasted vote drains to a hollow grey pip, so the imbalance of drained pips you scan is the efficiency gap. PACK and CRACK are two abuses that rob the majority on the very same voters; COMPACT collapses the gap to its proven minimum. A challenge deck carries a precomputed certificate for each card — a witness map, or an exhaustive proof of impossibility.
