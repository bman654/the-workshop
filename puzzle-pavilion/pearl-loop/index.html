◍ The Pearl Loop

Masyu — black & white pearls scattered on a grid pin exactly one closed loop, and you find it by drawing. Drag between cell-centres to lay the loop segment by segment: it must turn at every black pearl (running straight one cell on both arms) and run straight through every white pearl (turning just before or after). Never a guess: the pearls pin the loop to a single shape a logic solver threads from the forced turns outward.

The claim, self-tested

For every generated board: exactly one honouring loop (an exact loop-counter says so), reachable by pure deduction with no guess — forced turns & straights threaded outward from the pearls. Remove any one pearl and it breaks.

the deduction trace appears here when you Reveal logic
Why a loop you draw, not a chart. A Masyu board is a constraint you can see closing: each pearl is a local law on the loop's shape — black turns (with a straight arm each way), white passes straight (turning at a shoulder) — and together a handful of pearls admit a single closed curve. You drag the loop into being, illegal segments flush red, and the instant the one true loop satisfies every pearl it snaps closed with a flourish.
