# The Faithful Drum

> A wheel of life you spin. Doodle twelve frames, drop them in the brass drum, and drag it round —
> at the right speed the stills lock into a single moving thing.

A Victorian **zoetrope** you both MAKE and PLAY. Draw a twelve-frame loop on the strip (or nudge a
starter — a galloping horse, a bouncing ball, a blooming flower, a walk cycle, a flapping bird), then
grab the drum and spin. It carries real weight: fling it and it coasts down on its own friction,
dwelling a while in the **lock band** so you can sit in the moment it comes alive.

- **The illusion is honest, not faked.** The strip lines the FAR inner wall; thin slits are cut in the
  NEAR rim. Through each open slit you glimpse one vertical slice of the single frame directly
  opposite — the picture masked to darkness between slits. The frame you see steps exactly **one per
  slit-crossing**, and that clean step is what snaps the stills into smooth motion. All of it is driven
  from a continuous drum angle, so it reads smooth between refreshes and on low-refresh screens.
- **The governor** finds the lock for you: too slow is a carousel of stills, too fast a smear, and the
  green band in between is where it walks. **Reverse** gallops it backward; **Brake** stops it. A small
  warm bloom flares the instant it catches.
- **Draw with** four pen sizes, an eraser, an eight-colour palette (incl. an ink-out swatch), and
  **coloured onion-skin** — the frame before tinted cool, the frame after tinted warm — so a
  walk-cycle reads its own direction while you draw it.
- **Keep it.** Export your strip as an engraved contact sheet, or copy a compact seed and paste it
  back later (or load a saved `.txt`) to reload exactly what you made.

Deliberately claim-free: persistence-of-vision stays a whisper at the foot, never a proof — the
aliasing wing owns that theorem. This is pure Victorian-parlor craft, kin to the impossible atlases
and the poster press in the manor's Studies wing.

*Sound (optional, off until you touch it, honours the estate mute): a soft wooden bearing whir and a
faint per-slit tick at lock.*

The art (brass material, the ink starter loops, the sound bed) is greyboxed on purpose and forged
in-house by the estate's art foundry — see `art-specs/`.
