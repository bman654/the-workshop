Where the number comes from, and how to break it

The whole model is three sentences and none of them mentions a number of sides. Every film moves sideways at a speed equal to its curvature. A junction of three films slides downhill on total film length. And when a film gets short its ends swap, when a bubble gets tiny it goes. There is no pressure in it, no gas law, no area target.

the page and a half

for a bubble bounded by n arcs meeting at corners

  dA/dt  =  -∮κ ds            each film moves at κ
  ∮κ ds  =  2π - Σ(ext. angles)   Gauss-Bonnet, any closed curve
  ext. angle  =  π/3           IF that corner is 120°

  =>  dA/dt  =  n·π/3 - 2π  =  (π/3)(n - 6)

The 120° is the only place the 3 comes from, and it is not typed in anywhere — it is where the junction rule comes to rest, because three unit tensions can only sum to zero at 120°. This foam's corners find it on their own, to about one degree.

it really does not care how big the bubble is

rate against area, on a log scale, coloured by side count. The dashed lines are (π/3)(n−6). They are flat, which is the whole surprise: a five-sided bubble a thousand times larger dies just as fast.

hold the corners and it stops

Press hold the corners and the junctions are frozen where they stand. The films still flow by curvature — nothing else changed — but the corners can no longer find their 120°, and the exterior angles are no longer π/3. Watch the fit collapse. In the twin's run the rate per side falls from 1.0507 to 0.00596, R² from 0.998 to 0.19, and the whole foam goes still.

what is exactly true, and what is measured

Exact, at every instant, to machine precision: the bubbles tile a torus, so their areas sum to the same number forever (drift below 10⁻¹⁵ relative across a whole run), and Euler's formula forces the mean side count to be exactly six — 3V = 2E and V − E + F = 0 give F = V/2. Not approximately six. Six.

Measured, with a discretisation error: the rate per side. The two errors point opposite ways — a coarse film mesh reads the junction's tensions off chords and comes out steep, a long time step lags and comes out shallow — so the twin refines both together: (h, dt) = (0.20, 0.004) gives 1.043×π/3, (0.10, 0.002) gives 1.009, (0.05, 0.001) gives 1.008, against a seed-to-seed spread of ±0.007. The room runs the middle one.

And before any foam is asked anything, the same solver is pointed at a bare closed loop with no junctions in it at all, where the answer is exactly −2π whatever the shape — that is von Neumann's law at n = 0. It gets −6.2756 for a circle and −6.2662 for an ellipse.

A perfect honeycomb is frozen.

Every bubble on this copper has exactly six neighbours, and by the law in the corner of the room that means not one of them can change its area. It will sit like this forever. Nothing is wrong.

Give it a shove — swap a few neighbours, change nothing else, not one bubble's size — and it comes apart.
