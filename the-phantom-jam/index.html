The Phantom Jam

Cars on a one-lane loop, each watching only the gap to the car ahead — speed up when it opens, ease off when it closes. Crowd them past a critical density, tap one brake once, and a stop-and-go jam blooms out of nothing and crawls the wrong way round the ring, forever — though no car ever stops driving forward. Thin the traffic (or pack it tighter) and the same tap heals away. The jam is the readout. No graph.

Why a jam can have no cause

Each car relaxes its speed toward an optimal velocity set by its gap alone — vi ← vi + Δt·a·(V(hi) − vi), with V(h) a smooth crawl→cruise curve — then rolls forward xi ← xi + Δt·vi. A perfectly even ring is a fixed point: it stays smooth forever. But pack the cars so the equilibrium gap h* = L/N lands where the curve is steep — where 2·V′(h*) > a — and that smoothness is linearly unstable: one car's twitch grows as it passes down the line. The jam is a property of the crowd, not any one driver.

No number on the wall: near the band's lower edge the growth rate fades to zero, so whether a finite tap blooms over a finite watch is a soft, run-length dependent call — we paint a band you find on the dial, never a hard “jams above density X.” And it is two-sided: thin the traffic or pack it bumper to bumper and the steepness relents — the ring re-heals. The jam crawls backward because each car brakes a beat after the one ahead, so the front of the clot slides upstream even as every car creeps forward.

self-test —

The self-test proves what is EXACT: a tap at a jam-prone density blooms (CRUX-1); the same tap decays when too sparse AND when packed too tight (CRUX-2, the two-sided window); the jam crawls backward at a constant speed ≈ −0.58 car-lengths/s (CRUX-3); and the parameter-only threshold 2·V′(L/N) − a agrees with the simulation's observed grow/decay boundary, zero mismatches, the sharp upper edge flipping exactly where the band predicts (CRUX-4). We never pin a precise critical density — the lower edge is soft. The physics is single-sourced to core.mjs, byte-twinned into this page, with the Node twin core.test.mjs proving char-for-char parity.
