The Mirage

atmospheric refraction · the curving ray

Look straight down a desert highway at noon. The air skimming the scorching asphalt is hot and thin — its refractive index dips near the road — so a ray grazing toward the tar bends upward before it touches and reaches your eye from below the true horizon. You see a pool of sky lying on the road: false water that was never there, with the far car smeared into an inverted twin. Drag the thermometer up and watch the wet road that was never there bloom and creep toward you. Drag it to the bottom and it vanishes.

The two mirages
Drag HEAT to zero and every ray goes dead straight: the puddle and the flipped car vanish and the readout shows no false water. The mirage is provably the gradient, not a trick of the renderer. The wet-road distance the page prints is the certified turning-condition number — the same n(y)·cosθ that the self-test holds to a millionth.
