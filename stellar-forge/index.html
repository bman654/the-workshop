Where a star is weighed, not just watched. A star spends its whole life in a standoff — gravity pulling inward forever, fusion pushing back — and when the fuel runs out, the only thing left holding the core up is quantum degeneracy pressure. But that refusal has a ceiling. The Forge lets you put a dying core on the scales and add mass until the floor gives way. The body answers in light: it cools, it implodes, or it disappears. A second bench works the other end of a star's life — the fusion ladder that builds the elements up to iron and then refuses to climb further. A third follows the remnant past the end: a black hole is not cold and eternal — it glows, and by glowing it dies. A fourth steps out to cosmic scale: a whole spiral galaxy whose rotation won't fall the way the visible stars predict — and the only way to flatten the curve is to add mass you cannot see. And a fifth shows what a field does that a single force cannot: the gradient, not the strength, of a pull is the tide — release a ring and it deforms into a tidal egg; drag a moon past the Roche limit and it shears into a planetary ring. And a sixth finds the balance points: ride the frame that turns with a Sun and an Earth, and five places appear where the two pulls and the spin cancel — drag a probe, feel the field, and watch it hang on a gold dimple or loop a tadpole around L4.
