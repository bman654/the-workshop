Two compact stars in a circular orbit are not eternal. Relativity forces an accelerating binary to radiate — to pour orbital energy into gravitational waves, ripples in spacetime — and every ripple steals orbital energy, so the orbit shrinks. As the separation falls the orbit speeds up, a faster orbit radiates harder, harder radiation shrinks it faster: a chirp that rises until the two bodies touch and merge. Set mass A and mass B and a release separation, let them fall, and the binary spirals in on its own — you HEAR the pitch sweep up and SEE the clock tick down to coalescence. The note is set by the chirp mass M_c alone: swap the masses and the cartouche does not flicker. Throw the freeze the radiation toggle and the orbit becomes an eternal circle — no chirp, no merger.
