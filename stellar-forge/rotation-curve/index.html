Spin a spiral galaxy and ask how fast a star orbits at radius r. Newton answers in one line: a circular orbit is set entirely by the mass enclosed inside it, v(r) = √(G·M(<r)/r). Past the bright edge the visible stars run out, so the curve should fall away like the planets do — a Keplerian √(1/r) droop. The galaxies refuse: their curves go flat. A flat curve means M(<r) keeps growing — there is mass out there that gives off no light. The galaxy below turns by the same law you are trimming; flatten its curve by hand and find what it costs.
