The Tidal Field

the gradient is the tide
checking…

A body in orbit is never pulled by ONE force — it is pulled by a field that is stronger on the near side than the far. That difference, not the strength, is the tide. Two hands act it out on one tank around a single host well. Release a ring of free-falling beads — each a real point mass under the field at its own position — and the near bead outruns the far while the sides converge: the circle reshapes into a prolate egg, never inflating. Drag a moon inward and watch its own gravity fight the tide, frame by frame, until at the dashed Roche edge the tide wins and it shears into a ring. Then throw the uniform-field toggle — same pull, zero gradient — and both go dead.

Release the ring (R) or drag the moon across the Roche marker. The gradient is the only thing that makes a tide.
