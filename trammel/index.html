A real trammel of Archimedes, not a picture of one. Two slots cross at right angles; a rigid rod of length L carries two captive pins — one trapped in each slot. Pin the constraint and a pen fixed at station d along the rod has no choice: it traces a perfect ellipse, x²/a² + y²/b² = 1, where the semi-axes are exactly the two rod-segments, a = |L−d| (pen→far pin, on X) and b = |d| (pen→near pin, on Y). The curve is computed by the linkage, not plotted — a theorem in brass. Slide the bead to the rod's middle (d = L/2) and a = b: a circle. Slide it onto a pin and one axis vanishes: a line. It keeps the promise — the on-ellipse residual and an independent gardener's-string check both vanish to machine precision, and tilting a slot off square visibly breaks the figure off its ghost.

Witnesses & the break
Plate

Crank the rod by dragging the plate — let go and it coasts on its own momentum; grab it again to stop. Drag the bead (or the station slider) to morph the figure; it snaps to a circle at the rod's middle and to a line at either pin. Flip Tilt-a-slot and the slots stop being square: the hot trail peels off the dashed ghost ellipse and the fit residual blows up — the promise is keepable only when the slots are perpendicular.
