What you are operating

This is not a chart of a banked road — it is one, seen in iso cross-section. A car runs a circular curve of radius r whose roadbed is banked at θ. The only honest verb is the speed knob. As you drive, a free plumb-bob hanging from the cab roof reports the net sideways force you feel: drive too slow and gravity wins down the bank — the bob swings inboard; drive too fast and the centripetal demand wins — it swings up over the bank. At exactly one speed the two balance and the bob hangs dead-straight on the painted seat-normal. That speed is the readout.

The no-push speed — one number, and it's algebra

At the balance the road's normal force alone supplies the centripetal pull — no friction needed. Resolve forces along the bank: the down-slope term g·sinθ must equal the centripetal projection (v²/r)·cosθ. The mass cancels, and what's left is pure algebra: v² = g·r·tanθ, so v* = √(g·r·tanθ). The bob's angle off the seat-normal is atan2(v²/r·cosθ − g·sinθ,  g·cosθ + v²/r·sinθ), which is 0 exactly at v=v* and crosses zero just once — so a round has one clean answer. The self-test measures that null at 1e-16 radians.

Load the car — the played red herring

Flip load the car between a light cab and a loaded freight wagon. Watch the answer: it does not move. There is no mass in v* = √(g·r·tanθ) nor in the bob angle — the heavy car needs more centripetal force and gets more normal force, in exact proportion. Same bank, same lock speed, whether you're carrying feathers or pig iron. The self-test asserts the two are byte-identical at every sample.

Flatten the track — the load-bearing control

Pull flatten the track and the bank goes to θ=0. Now there is no answer: a flat road offers no normal-force component to turn you, so no speed nulls the bob — speed up and it only swings further out. The scoreboard reads NO LOCK — flat track has no no-push speed; v*=0. The only "null" is v=0: not driving. That's the neg-control you can feel — a banked track has a single secret speed; a flat one has none.

Widen the grip — from a hairline to a band

Real tyres grip, so flip widen the grip and a green holds-without-sliding band blooms around the hairline: every speed from √(g·r·(tanθ−μ)/(1+μ·tanθ)) (about to slide down) to √(g·r·(tanθ+μ)/(1−μ·tanθ)) (about to slide up) holds. Crank μ→0 and watch the band collapse to the single hairline v* — the played proof that the no-push speed is the band's pinch-point. Bank steeply enough (μ·tanθ≥1) and the band has NO UPPER LIMIT — grip holds at any speed.

Honesty

The bob's settled angle is the exact core value; only the swing-in (the critically-damped ease toward equilibrium) is decorative. Idealized: point-mass car, rigid bank, a free pendulum reading the steady seat-frame force. The show the forces overlay constructs the actual force triangle out of the scene — mg down, the seat-normal N, their sum — and snaps = mv²/r when the sum's horizontal component equals the centripetal demand, i.e. at the null. Every readout traces to one inlined core, proven byte-identical to the Node twin.
