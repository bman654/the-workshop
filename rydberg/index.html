Measure the Rydberg constant from noisy starlight. A blind line-fitter that knows zero atomic physics reads R off four smudged hydrogen lines — plot 1/λ against u=1/n² and the Balmer law is a straight line, slope −R, intercept R/4. It names the constant twice, and the two must agree.

These are air wavelengths. The fit is self-consistent (R from slope == R from intercept) and R²≈1 — yet R is +277 ppm off CODATA. Consistency ≠ correctness: Rydberg lives in vacuum.

The band — ±t·SE (n=4 ⇒ 2 d.o.f.)

With only four points and two fitted params there are 2 degrees of freedom — Student-t, not normal. A naive ±1·SE band covers just ~57.5%; the t-band is the honest one.
