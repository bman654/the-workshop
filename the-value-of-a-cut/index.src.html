The Value of a Cut

A tangle of coloured edges grows up from the earth. Blue edges are yours, red the opponent's. Cut one of your own — it, and everything now floating, falls away. The whole tangle collapses to a single fraction, and that one number, alone, foretells who wins. Watch the oracle call it before you move — then play it out and see it hold.

The oracle just mis-called — on purpose. An all-green Hackenbush is impartial: either player may cut any edge, so its worth is a nimber (the ⊕ of stalk-heights), not a number. The value plate reads ∗? because there is no dyadic to print; the bird warns the sign lies here. Only blue/red partisan colour makes a position a genuine number whose sign is the winner. This is real combinatorial-game theory diverging — not a bug.
What you are watching. Every Blue-Red Hackenbush position has a Conway surreal value { L | R } — reduced by the simplicity rule to the plainest number lying between the best Blue can reach and the best Red can reach. A lone blue edge on the earth is +1; a blue edge grounded with a red one stacked on top is +½; each further red above halves it again — ¼, ⅛, … — the dyadic fractions, the first rung of Conway's numbers (the Colon Principle: read the stalk root→sky in binary). The sign of that one number is the winner: positive → Blue wins, negative → Red wins, zero → whoever moves next loses — no matter who starts.
Honesty. The value is computed by exact surreal / dyadic arithmetic — an integer (BigInt) numerator over a power of two, no floating point anywhere; a code-disjoint negamax is the independent who-wins authority, and the self-test proves their signs agree on thousands of random trees with zero mismatches. The all-green mode is an impartial (Nim) game where the theory genuinely differs — a nimber, not a number — so the oracle's mis-call there is the honest answer, not a defect.
