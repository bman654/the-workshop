📐The Parallax Baseline

Drag Earth across its one-AU baseline and blink the sky between January and July: the near star wobbles against the fixed far field. Read its half-angle p on the brass micrometer, then throw RESOLVE — the parsec definition turns it into a distance, d = 1 / p. Push the star to infinity (or lock the baseline) and the throw honestly vanishes.

What you are doing

The angle is real and it is tiny — even the nearest star shifts by less than an arcsecond. That is the whole reason astronomers needed a baseline as wide as Earth's orbit and a micrometer this fine. The sky × knob only magnifies the picture so your eye can catch the wobble; it never changes the angle the micrometer reads. Lock baseline and push to ∞ are the two honest negative controls — no baseline, or no nearby star, means no parallax at all.
