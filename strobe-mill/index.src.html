Grab the brass wheel and crank it — drag it round, or fling and let go; friction bleeds it down. Then dial the strobe. When the flash rate matches the spin the wheel freezes — dead still, though a faint under-ghost shows it is really still turning. Nudge the flash a hair above and the frozen wheel crawls backward; a hair below and it crawls forward. Flash at twice the spin and the one bright pip splits into a two-pip ghost. This is the Tone Mill's stillness — the same rate, watched instead of heard.

A strobe lamp is a shutter for the eye: it only lets you see the wheel at the instants it flashes. Flash at the spin rate and every flash lands the wheel in the same place — persistence of vision fuses the still frames into one steady image, so the wheel reads frozen. This is the very same aliasing that lets a strobe read a rate: the flash rate that freezes the wheel is the spin. Add spokes and a lower flash can freeze the spokes while the bright pip still hops — proof the wheel is turning even when its pattern stands still.
