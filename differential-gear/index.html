For equal-tooth side gears the carrier turns at the exact average of the two inputs — the Willis epicyclic law. Proven three ways so it isn’t one formula checking itself: the carrier equals the half-sum across a sweep, the three solve-directions cross-check, and the lock breaks it red. Machine-exact (<1e−12).

This is the equal-tooth speed-average only. A real car’s open differential uses equal teeth too, so the same law holds for its wheel speeds — but torque split, limited-slip, Torsen worm gearing, and unequal-tooth planetaries are another story, and this bench does not claim them.
