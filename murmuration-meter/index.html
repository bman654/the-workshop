Why the flock loses its mind

Each bird turns to the average heading of every neighbour within a short radius, then takes a random kick of size η — θi ← mean∡(neighbours) + (rng−½)·η — and steps forward at a fixed speed. How aligned the whole flock is reads off one number, the order φ = |Σv̂| / N: near 1 every bird flies the same way (one mind), near 1/√N the headings are scattered round the circle (a milling crowd). Turn η up and the kick drowns the copying — agreement collapses, φ falls.

No number on the wall: the place it tips is a soft crossover — it slides with how many birds there are and how densely they are packed — so we let you find it by hand on the dial, we do not paint a precise ηc on the wall. The honest test: make the birds blind (each ignores its neighbours) and no amount of low noise will order them — φ sits on the floor at every η. The order is the copying, not the motion.

self-test —

The self-test proves what is EXACT: the order φ is rotation-blind (turn the whole flock and φ does not move), the two ends anchor (η=0 ⇒ φ→1; η=2π ⇒ φ·√N is O(1) at two flock sizes — the 1/√N floor is a law, not a point), and φ(η) only ever falls as the noise rises. We never claim a precise ηc — the crossover is soft, density- and N-dependent, crossed by eye.
