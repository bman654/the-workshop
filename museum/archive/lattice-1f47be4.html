Self-test — the proof

Now line the wells up in a row, repeated forever — a crystal. The single atom's sharp levels can no longer stay sharp: each one smears into a BAND of allowed energies, and between the bands open forbidden GAPS no electron may have. Whether a solid is a metal, a semiconductor or an insulator is decided entirely by where the bands and gaps fall — the deepest reason matter is the way it is.

A single atom holds sharp levels; bring atoms together into a crystal and every level broadens into a band wide enough to hold one state per atom. Crank P high (atoms held far apart) and the bands shrink back toward those isolated atomic levels; drop it toward 0 (the free electron) and the gaps slam shut. Fill the bands with electrons and the punchline appears: a band left half-full conducts — a metal; bands filled exactly to a gap cannot — an insulator; a thin gap is a semiconductor. The self-test proves the band edges two independent ways and counts the states a finite ring really holds.
