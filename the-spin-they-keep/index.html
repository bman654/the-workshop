Same conserved quantity, same consequence — closer means faster. But not one shared magnitude: the skater's L and the planet's L keep their own scales and units (≈12× apart, different units). What's shared is that neither budges. The needle reads only the dimensionless held-fraction — never a number equated across worlds.

Push a world and watch its rate climb while the needle sits still — a shrinking radius exactly offsets the rising rate, so L is kept. Then flip a neg-control: a friction brake on the pivot, or an off-centre tug on the orbit. Now an external torque acts, that world's L bleeds away, the shared minimum falls, and the needle sags off HELD — the two worlds that moved as one now move apart. Flip it back and the needle snaps home.
